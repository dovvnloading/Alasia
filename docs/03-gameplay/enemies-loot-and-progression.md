# Opposition, itemization, progression and economy

**ID:** SYS-003 · **Status:** WORKING · **Depends on:** WRL-001, SYS-001–002 · **Version:** 0.1

## 1. Every enemy needs an ecological, human or mechanical reason

Alasia's enemy density comes from converging pressures at **faults, tide gates, quarries, roads being evacuated, and actively loaded work sites**. Most villagers are not combatants and should not become target dummies to sustain loot. The player kills hostile organisms, disables unsafe machines and fights organized crews where a concrete conflict demands it.

### Encounter families

| Family | Origin and behavior | Combat identity | Telegraph language | Where it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bridleshells** | Territorial shell fauna disturbed by new ground pulses | Fast rushers, armored fronts, weaker sides; packs with one heavier charger | Shell crouch / scraped forward wedge; scraping audible + visible | Orren mudcuts, broken quay foundations |
| **Chalk colonies** | Mineralizing fissure organisms growing under cyclic loading | Small linked bodies emerging along strain seams; area denial, vibration response | Pale branching ground cracks before emergence; porous collapses | South Cut, Keld galleries, Sere foundations |
| **Held** | Workers trapped in failed harness response cycles, intermittently aware | Slow powerful repetitive strokes, dangerous proximity; possible disable interactions | Tool arm ratchets twice, local brace glows white at fracture | Maintenance chambers, unsafe historic rigs |
| **Contract crews** | Paid guards and service workers protecting restricted controls | Formation defense, sappers, ranged bolted tools, shields with genuine limits | Hand signals, exposed winding gear, visible deployment | Verran archives, Selwick control routes |
| **Unseated machinery** | Live jacks, pistons and guide mechanisms in overload | Environmental boss components, cyclic arena pressure | Repeated stroke sounds, moving shadows, mechanical gauge | South Cut / Outer Sill exclusively |

### Enemy design constraints

**Bridleshells:** not just crabs scaled to boss size. The twin raised straps across the foreplate resemble a ferry bridle: this is the boat crews' name, not an invented ancient taxonomy. Design one locomotion grammar: heavy foreplates, flexible pale rear joints, fast lateral recovery, vibration-sensitive stance. Variants arise from biology (young flanking runner, mature plating, nest defender), not elemental color swaps. They may retreat or defend nests.

**Chalk colonies:** not fungi with green poison clouds or skeletal undead. The colony is many rigid mineral threads grown around small living organisms; threatening motion is a coordinated **surface ripple** visible before eruption. A colony can lose its connection when a fitted surface is broken.

**Held:** the player can often disable a repeating harness stroke, break its collar or force a nonfatal collapse. Avoid implying that injured workers have become irredeemable monsters. In combat, players still need quick, understandable solutions; empathy is delivered through aftermath, unique VO and occasional practical rescue choices, not hidden pacifism penalties.

**Contract crews:** limited numbers, normal concerns, wages and families. They attack when the player endangers a protected route or enters a sealed worksite after warnings. Some flee, surrender or help when access is formally authorized. No infinite “evil soldier spawn.”

## 2. The first encounter roster (candidate)

- **Bridleshell Scout:** light charger; teaches dodge from a forward wedge; vulnerable after missing.
- **Bridleshell Mason:** broad foreplate; flank or break its footing; first stagger tutorial.
- **Chalk Fissure:** several small organisms spring from a marked connected crack; teaches terrain observation.
- **Chalk Crown:** stationary colony attachment projects a short traveling ridge; teaches lane changes.
- **Overloaded Rig:** anchored maintenance frame with predictable alternating slam; destroy/vent fitting after opening.
- **Service Sapper:** human who prepares a safe but dangerous bolt-line; interrupts long setup and telegraphs the shot.

The **Orren slice** should make 3–4 of these excellent rather than ship an entire atlas of interchangeable swarms. A normal family needs distinct size/silhouette, movement rhythm, attack range, vulnerability and loot rationale.

## 3. Boss design as a dramatic, mechanical argument

**South Cut apparatus (slice boss):** a pressurizing donor shunt that the player must stop from unloading into the quay. It has a readable cyclic sequence:
1. **Load:** the collars brighten at fractures; narrow lane threats force movement.
2. **Transfer:** a piston pushes through marked ground; safe shoulders appear.
3. **Vent:** the machine exposes a damageable fitting; attacks and class signatures punish it.
4. **Failure escalation:** one safe shoulder collapses, reshaping the next cycle—not an arbitrary one-hit cutscene.

A Linewright can use pylons as route points, a Shorer can catch a stroke to redirect Counterload, a Sounder can read timing; **all** can damage and interact with the exposed vent. No profession gate. Successful combat buys a *temporary hold*, not a magical cure.

**Final boss — the Sixth Jack, Outer Sill:** a multi-part lift assembly under overload while defending crews and displaced organisms complicate access. Phase transition visibly changes exactly which receiving/donor circuit is engaged, causing changing arena geometry. Between phases, the player must vent or reset real collars. The final plan is enacted **after** the fight by people and procedure, not by gaining the boss's soul.

**Boss acceptance:** first death can be explained by an intelligible tell; readable patterns under 60fps; tactical counterplay for all three professions; failure not dependent on recognizing one unmarked safe pixel; phase transitions do not reset the physical rules.

## 4. Loot: tools and repairable history, not infinite magic junk

**Items should be attractive as playstyle decisions and coherent as objects.** Use profession implements, working harnesses, portable collars, fittings, boots/gloves and distinctive keepsakes—not a random “ancient sword of everything” dropped by every shell animal.

**Item provenance:**
- Fauna: hides, plates, reagents and mechanically useful organic parts, **not complete fabricated weapons**.
- Colonies: mineral growths and compromised seamglass fragments.
- Crews/rigs: fitted tools, stamped collars, gauge components and salvaged equipment.
- Unique authored rewards: heirloom work pieces tied to character or institution, with a documented designer or user and an interpretable mechanical effect.
- Chests and stores: inventories that make sense for their site and history, seeded by loot tables without pretending every shelf was filled just for the hero.

**Candidate inventory structure:** primary implement, harness, collar, two functional fittings, footwear, limited carry/salvage capacity. Final slot count must follow UX testing. Avoid constant replacement of visually iconic work tools; customize key parts while preserving each profession's silhouette.

## 5. Item design grammar

Every affix/fitting is tagged to a **verb or material behavior**: **hold**, **receive**, **release**, **read**, **return**, **connect**, **sever**, **travel**, **grounded**, **fracture**. Numbers may tune performance, but the best drops alter timing, reach, geometry, or risk.

**Example authored fittings (tests, not finalized loot):**

| Fitting | Clear behavioral change | Tradeoff | Benefits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sliding Heel** | Shorer can drag a stake through one turn without resetting it | Stored Counterload cap reduced | Mobile offensive Shorer |
| **Narrow Mouth** | Sounder True Return passes through a second correctly read target on same line | Shorter read duration | Pack precision |
| **Hard Reeve** | Linewright lines survive one heavy impact and can be used for a quicker second traversal | Lower damage transferred through line | Mobile Linewright |
| **Dry Collar** | Common tool fitting resists short water interruption once | Longer recovery after discharge | Route planning across classes |
| **Cross-grained Foot** | Firm footing on soft stone but reduced peak force | Lower burst capacity | Class-specific terrain adaptation |

A fitting must not secretly multiply damage on every skill with no corresponding tactical change. A character's viability must not depend on one ultra-rare drop.

## 6. Progression philosophy and build shape

**Starter goal:** players learn their profession loop before encountering deep customization. Class skills unlock a few alternative *uses of the same mechanics*, and equipped fittings produce deliberate build identities. Meaningful growth should be visible as “I now make enemies move and take damage differently,” not just “+400% to all damage.”

**Proposed first-slice depth:** a short level band (roughly 1–10), 4–6 meaningful active buttons, three class archetype tests and a small pool of craftable fittings. Campaign scope and level curve are undecided; the demo does **not** imply a 100-level game.

**Reward rhythm:** routine fights provide salvage and maintenance parts; a difficult authored fight should reliably advance a build path; named site rewards carry an identifiable physical reason. Avoid grind-based gating of main story evidence.

**Crafting:** re-fit collars and components at existing workshops using legible material recipes and work benches. Provide previews of which skill's geometry will change. No high-friction slot machine, gambling pressure or duplicate-rarity inflation before the core loot loop is fun.

**Rarity hypothesis:** ordinary → fitted → commissioned → singular provenance. Rarity primarily indicates behavior variation, workmanship and story—not simply brightness or 10x stats. “Singular provenance” requires design attention and meaningful limits; no infinite copies implied in canon, even if a game run can replay the item drop.

## 7. Equipment economy and public trust

All four standing islands participate in repair supply:
- Keld mines and calibrates components.
- Orren produces ropes, ferries and agricultural materials.
- Selwick maintains high-capacity collars.
- Verran certifies and taxes major fittings.

The player cannot collect a thousand seamglass engines from a field of crabs without breaking the economy. Rare materials should be concentrated at quarries, sills, salvaged wrecks and regulated workshops.

**Trading or live player-to-player markets are not in the first slice.** Balancing against a future online economy prematurely would consume time better spent on combat quality and authored gear.

## 8. Long-tail temptation and strict restraint

No procedural endless dungeon, live seasonal treadmill, sprawling crafting-tree simulation or monetization-driven reward cadence is approved. They could be researched after the campaign and item loop demonstrate quality. A polished 10-minute fight with mechanically interesting loot is more valuable now than a thousand meaningless drops.

## 9. Systems review questions

- Does a Shorer's crowd clear come from an actually different geometry than a Sounder's timed shock or a Linewright's line snap?
- Can a player understand a fatal attack in a black-and-white capture at half-resolution?
- Can a boss be beaten with starter gear by a skilled player and with reasonable assists by a newer player?
- Does each loot item have a plausible origin *here* and a non-cosmetic reason to be exciting?
- Are Held encounters humanly considered without making the combat feel like a lecture?
- Do monsters remain dangerous because of behavior rather than stat inflation?
- Does a class retain its identity without item sets or legendary drops?

If not, revise the systemic design before populating another zone.
