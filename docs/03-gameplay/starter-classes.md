# Starter professions — three genuinely different combat grammars

**ID:** SYS-002 · **Status:** EXPERIMENTAL / prototype specifications · **Version:** 0.1

The profession names and kits are **candidates that must win mechanical blind tests**. A profession is not original merely because it has a new noun. Each must pass four challenges: **new decision loop**, **unique positioning**, **distinct failure/recovery**, and **clear reason to exist in Alasia**. They are trained people, not bloodline magic users or superhero castes. Any body type can play any profession.

## Comparison at a glance

| Profession | Civilian origin | Combat verb | Primary resource | Positioning question | What mastery looks like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shorer** | Shoreline bracing engineer | **Bear → return** | Counterload (stored response to contact) | *Where can I establish and move footing?* | Catch a dangerous commitment, reroute its impact, then collapse the bracing geometry offensively |
| **Sounder** | Tide and foundation surveyor | **Read → answer** | Readings (per-enemy tuned intervals) | *Where is the next opening and what surface carries my return?* | Recognize several enemy beats, strike during windows and convert precision into controlled crowd breaks |
| **Linewright** | Ferry-line, conduit and salvage rigger | **Connect → redistribute** | Tension (force in a temporary line network) | *Whom or what should I connect, and where will they move?* | Make moving enemies drive damage and control through linked physical lines |

**Anti-clone test:** a Shorer with a sword and “rage” fails. A Sounder with wand projectiles and “mana” fails. A Linewright with stealth, traps and bow combos fails. Their *input decisions* must remain distinct even with all meshes replaced by gray cylinders.

## 1. Shorer — a frontline mechanic who weaponizes support

**Professional logic:** Shore workers install sprung jacks and brace feet in failing walls. A Shorer recognizes *where* a building will take a load, plants temporary supports and transfers impact away from the brittle point. On a battlefield, the worker is not a tank carrying a magical shield; the ground and the placed brace are the apparatus.

**Object language:** long pinned hammer with hinged footplate; two spring-steel brace stakes; weight gauge mounted where the actor can see it. Inscribed, functional bearing surfaces; scarred wrists and deliberate steps. Silhouette broad at the **tool**, not just oversized human shoulders.

**Exclusive spatial mechanic — Footing:** the Shorer may maintain **up to two** visible brace stakes seated in valid firm ground. With the player, they define short support edges / a triangle; attack and guard outcomes change when an incoming force intersects this geometry. Stakes are destructible/repositionable; mud lowers reliability unless a skill or fitting provides a proper base. Never auto-build permanent walls. The player can fight effectively with one or zero stakes.

**Resource — Counterload (candidate 0–100):** gain by striking a braced target, meeting an incoming attack with a timed facing guard, and interacting with live ground pulses. Spend on returns and repositioning. Some load leaks when out of engagement or when a stake shatters. **You can build load by attacking; you're never required to be hit.**

**Proposed kit (not full shipping roster):**

| Action | Physical expression | Reward | Real limitation / counterplay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Heel Strike** · primary | Short forward hammer wedge drives a steel foot into ground | Reliable fast contact; modest Counterload per hit | Short reach; committed angle |
| **Set Foot** · active | Plant or redeploy a sprung stake at target ground | Creates support edge, narrows enemy approach | Valid footing and max-two limit; fast action, not setup ordeal |
| **Receive** · active | Brief forward brace catches an active impact | Strong Counterload and stagger on well-timed contact | Facing/timing matter; cannot nullify all multi-directional damage |
| **Return Stroke** · active | Release held load as a broad ground shear / follow brace edges | Main satisfying crowd clear; optional line propagation | Costs load; weak when overused without buildup |
| **Drag Brace** · active | Shift an existing stake while sweeping the intervening edge | Mobile reorientation, controlled displacement, escape angle | Exposes old geometry; cannot pull anchored bosses |
| **Failure Line** · signature spend | Intentionally fold current bracing network into a travelling fault | High spectacle burst with route choice and strong contact | Consumes large Counterload, collapses stakes, high recovery |

**Example 8-second engagement:** a cluster approaches from a corridor. The Shorer Heel Strikes through the front two, plants a stake against an existing wall, receives a clearly telegraphed ram at the edge, swings Return Stroke through the committed pack, drags the stake behind a flanker, then spends the remaining load rather than passively standing still. Success is *reorienting the fight*, not being immobile.

**Intended strengths:** visible crowd breaks, defensive counterpressure, robust but deliberate space control. **Costs:** poor reach in deep loose ground, vulnerable from behind when overcommitted, lower precision on a nimble single target without careful stance.

**Build expression:** **Bearing** fittings enlarge guard-return opportunities but reduce movement reach; **Sliding Foot** fittings make reposition quick but reduce stored capacity; **Split Heel** turns released energy into two narrow wedges rather than one fan. Each changes play, has an explicit tradeoff and respects two-stake cap.

**Skill ceiling / failure mode:** if the optimal strategy is “put stakes down and wait to be hit,” this class has failed. If all its bursts are just colored hammer shockwaves, it has failed. Prototype moving geometry and an attack-only load route before art.

## 2. Sounder — precision based on observing an attack, not chanting

**Professional logic:** a surveyor checks undersea depth and foundation integrity by lowering a weighted plumb and striking fitted rods. Subtle changes in returning vibration show the state of the material. In a fight the Sounder doesn't hear prophecy or hurl notes through empty air: they *measure a coming commitment*, then send a matching physical impulse through tool and substrate when the opponent is vulnerable.

**Object language:** a reel-mounted sounding weight, twin calibration rods, measuring cords, stamped interval plates at the belt. Thin, deliberate gestures; quick wrists, mobile stance. Visual notes are expanding short concentric *contact rings*, not colorful sound-magic circles.

**Exclusive temporal mechanic — Readings:** enemy tells contain one or more **observable mechanical phases** (windup, commit, recovery). The Sounder records a target's interval by inspecting a tell with a sounding pulse or hitting during a meaningful response window. Readings are **per target** (candidate cap three, short expiry), representing calibrated knowledge; spending one times a heavier strike to the target's exposed motion. No time stop, audio-only rhythm minigame or passive buff stack that resembles mana.

**Enemy-read baseline:** a basic hit can establish an imperfect first reading on a slow or idle target, so fighting trash remains fluid; precise timing makes it stronger/faster, not mandatory. Timing information appears as a clear shape around the target or in its motion path, not at a HUD edge.

**Proposed kit:**

| Action | Physical expression | Reward | Real limitation / counterplay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Plumb Cast** · primary | Throw and recall a tethered weighted head across a short line | Reliable mid-range damage, basic read on contact / return | Tether length and obstacle path; recall timing |
| **Take Sounding** · active | Brief rod-to-ground tap reads a local contact region | Reveals useful tell intervals; primes several nearby enemies | Must contact contiguous material; limited cone/radius |
| **True Return** · active | Strike along a marked target's predicted movement line | Quick, precise high-impact answer; spends Reading | Loses burst if fired too early/late; still meaningful base hit |
| **Skip Count** · active | Lateral step-and-sounding tap at prior foot position | Evasion, coaxes vibration-seeking enemies into an opening | Not invisibility or teleport; step has actual path |
| **Bell Break** · active | Counterpulse against an actively committed attack | Interrupts lesser foe; meaningful boss poise damage | Narrower timing, reading cost; no permanent stun chain |
| **Underheard** · signature spend | Release a sequence of tuned impulses along a connected grounded surface | High clarity AoE on correctly read pack, concentrated boss pressure | Requires earned readings; breaks at water gap / unfitted surface |

**Example 8-second engagement:** two Bridleshells charge while a larger work-encrusted creature lifts a plate. The Sounder taps Take Sounding to see approach intervals, steps off the charge line, catches a Bridleshell on the weight's return, uses True Return on the large foe's committed lift, then times Bell Break before the plate drops. The encounter has **attention**, not an invisible rhythm meter.

**Intended strengths:** high single-target ceiling, reliable telegraph punish, graceful mid-range counterplay, information-rich enemy encounters. **Costs:** weak when forced into erratic blind firing; needs clear silhouettes and not merely numerical buffs; pressure on multiple flanks.

**Build expression:** **Short Count** affords wider reads but smaller payoffs; **Deep Reading** rewards maintained focus on one armored target; **Broken Return** causes a deliberate narrow shatter cone on a correctly timed strike. Each has explicit lost flexibility.

**Skill ceiling / failure mode:** if Sounder does no meaningful damage unless the player succeeds at an onerous QTE, reject it. If a Sounder can simply spam wide AoE on cooldown, reject it. In silent-mode playtests, timing cues must still be obvious.

## 3. Linewright — combat as a live, destructible connection

**Professional logic:** ferry lines and buried conductive routes need riggers who can join, shorten, cross and release cable under load. A Linewright is a salvage specialist capable of making *other actors' movement become the engine of the fight*. They do not summon minions, vanish into shadow or plant passive minefields.

**Object language:** coiled flat cable, paired lead hooks, spool crank, ceramic guide rings, knife for emergency severance. Clear thread silhouette between hand and terrain; motion emphasizes horizontal sweeps and rapid reeling. Lines are industrial materials and physically anchored, not magical lasers.

**Exclusive relational mechanic — Working line:** up to **three nodes** and **two live edges** (candidates). A node can attach to valid terrain or an enemy with a mass limit; edges have actual line of sight, a defined maximum length, and clear cut/break behavior. The Linewright can use one edge to matter in under a second; three-node setups are optional mastery, not mandatory prep.

**Resource — Tension (candidate 0–100):** generate as connected targets move against a taut line, as the player traverses an edge, or with deliberate reeling and basic attacks. Spend on pulls, force split and snap. A fixed boss may be an anchor but does not get dragged. Physical force transfer respects mass and an explicit diminishing cap.

**Proposed kit:**

| Action | Physical expression | Reward | Real limitation / counterplay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lead Throw** · primary | Fast weighted hook hit; may establish moving node | Direct damage and first attach without extra setup action | Short-to-mid range; non-attachable armor shown clearly |
| **Tie Off** · active | Drive terrain peg or connect a lead to another valid node | Create line immediately; define fight's geometry | Collision, distance, node cap; preview before commit |
| **Reeve** · active | Pull self along a live line at visible, finite speed | Aggressive gap-close and retreat along an existing route | Can be intercepted; blocked route snaps or stops safely |
| **Draw In** · active | Reel two movable attachments toward a shared point | Cluster enemies, interrupt weak rushes, gain Tension | Heavy/anchored targets resist displacement but still strain the cable |
| **Cross-load** · active | Divert a bounded portion of one hit to another connected target | Rewards smart target choice and position | Damage does not recurse; caps prevent infinite duplication |
| **Snap Cast** · signature spend | Release stored tension; line whips along a readable path | High-density finish or defensive cut | Consumes Tension and destroys affected edge(s) |

**Example 8-second engagement:** attach a lead to the first charging Bridleshell, tie it to a stone foot on the opposite side of the path, use Reeve to relocate as the animal crosses, and spend tension to snap the edge through its pack. Then anchor a larger foe as an immovable pivot and pull small creatures toward it. The enemy's motion supplies the attack's shape.

**Intended strengths:** dynamic AoE routing, control of pursuit, fast reposition along chosen geometry, skill expression in target selection. **Costs:** weak in open, bare water with few anchors, lines susceptible to enemy cuts, higher spatial planning burden than Shorer.

**Build expression:** **Long Reeve** favors travel and lower transferred damage; **Hard Splice** boosts split/line resilience but shortens maximum edge; **Open Loop** enables an extra defensive sever option at cost of lower peak damage. Do not simply add a fourth node because it's numerically stronger.

**Skill ceiling / failure mode:** if players spend the fight wrestling target-select UI rather than pulling lines, reject or simplify. If rope effects occlude incoming damage or exceed performance budgets, reduce simultaneous edges. If a dash through a wall is possible, the class has violated R-03.

## 4. Shared kit conventions — just enough to teach, not to homogenize

- **Everyone can:** walk, evade, strike immediately, generate resources while attacking, interrupt some lighter foes, and use emergency terrain interaction.
- **Everyone can face:** a mobile enemy, armored enemy, swarm and machine set piece without class-exclusive story locks.
- **No player needs:** a specific legendary to make their starter loop function, a pet micromanager, a universal rage bar, five stacked currencies or compulsory long setup.
- **Shared gear families:** implement, harness, collar, fittings and travel pieces, but the same fitting tag can express differently within each profession. See [loot](enemies-loot-and-progression.md).
- **Player readability:** Shorer = support edges and wide grounded cracks; Sounder = interval ticks on opponent silhouettes and precise weighted returns; Linewright = actual taut physical lines between nodes. A screenshot with no labels should distinguish them.

## 5. Starter progression, not predetermined skill-tree bloat

**Levels 1–3:** core attack, one signature tool interaction, evade. **Levels 4–6:** first resource spend + one counter or movement alternative. **Levels 7–10:** a mutually exclusive functional fitting and a major class signature. The exact levels and timing are experimental; the **sequence of learning** is the commitment.

Each profession should support **three viable build intentions** (survival/control, precision/bossing, mobile pack clear) without becoming another profession in disguise. Prove with a small authored item pool first. A sprawling talent graph is prohibited until all three loops are enjoyable with no advanced items.

**Class origin scenes:** profession-specific observation/line of dialogue and unique tutorial prop, then merge into the same canonical emergency. Armor and silhouette are tied to work history, not fantasy gender stereotypes.

## 6. How they should feel different to play

A Shorer is most interesting when the battlefield shifts around two moving support points; standing still and waiting to be struck would drain the class of identity. A Sounder should make a dangerous enemy's rhythm *understandable* and let the player answer it without turning combat into a rhythm-game exam. A Linewright should make a connection useful immediately, then let mastery reveal surprising routes and moving-target combinations.

These ideas belong in the hands of players with simple prototype models as soon as development begins. The essential question is whether a player *makes different decisions* with each profession—not whether three character sheets use different names.

## 7. Known originality risks — deliberately not disguised

**Shorer** could become a “barbarian with deployable shield.” Preserve the stake geometry and *redirected load* identity, keep mobile offensive options, and remove passive stand-and-block incentives.

**Sounder** could become a renamed ranged spellcaster or frustrating rhythm class. Preserve per-target *observation/response*, physical plumb range, and non-audio tells. No AoE beam from nowhere.

**Linewright** could become a generic trapper/rogue or a technical control nightmare. Preserve direct *connections between moving actors*, immediate first line and visible force flow. No stealth, endless traps or infinite recursive damage.

Names remain provisional as the world is written and the classes begin to move. Avoid associations with established games, and change any name or ability that turns out ordinary, unclear or derivative.
