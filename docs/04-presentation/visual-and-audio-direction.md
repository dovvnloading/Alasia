# Visual, interface and audio direction — a world carved by load

**ID:** ART-001 · **Status:** WORKING / visual brief v0.1 · **Dependencies:** WRL-001–003, SYS-001–003

## 1. The look in one image

**A low harbor at dusk after the ground has fallen:** ash-black wall feet exposed beneath wet chalk lines; brass gauges still mounted at their former level; a damaged ferry under a pale rain sky; a few distant windows burning amber; below a half-broken stone stair, the white hairline of an overstressed seamglass collar. A worker with a recognizably functional tool stands where a measured shore ought to be.

**Desired genre impression:** dark medieval/ancient 3D top-down ARPG, rich grounded materials, hostile weather, dramatic lighting, polished animation and brutal readable combat. **Our identity:** industrial-civic dread in a preindustrial island basin. The *world* is threatening because it has a material history. Skulls, gothic arches and blood-lit cathedrals are not default art direction.

## 2. Art pillars with observable rules

**A1 — Gravity is the protagonist.** Walls are battered at their feet, horizontal courses refuse to meet, tension wires bow, every deck and terrace shows how it is held up. If a set looks cool without suggesting what loads it carries, revise it.

**A2 — The coastline remembers.** Waterlines, salt blooms, stripped paint, warped tide posts, broken masonry and surviving repairs tell what happened. A building can reveal two historical eras in one vertical section.

**A3 — Work makes clothing.** Crews wear tools, patched waxed cloth, leather, iron and bronze fittings appropriate to rank and need. Distinguish civilian class and worker profession through *use*, not elaborate generic fantasy armor.

**A4 — The unnatural is rare and exact.** Seamglass is black/dark crystalline material with visible pale stress veins, normally unlit; during a loaded event it briefly scatters a dull cold luminance along existing fractures. No permanent neon magic field.

**A5 — Readability beats atmospheric obscurity.** Critical silhouettes, attack shapes and player-owned control geometry must survive dark backgrounds, fog and spell density. Black is an accent, not permission to hide the game.

Warm light indicates *human habitation or controlled work*, not generic “good faction” coloring. Hazard red is a safety language and must have shape support.

## 4. Place-specific visual grammar

**Orren — the repaired low shore**
- Low horizontal clay/reed fields cut by darker channels; few tall landmarks.
- Quay posts patched with mismatched stone feet, rope stains, flood-marked plaster and reed bundles caught at higher levels.
- Roofs of waxed wood, straw and terracotta fragments over stone; irregular repetition is functional repair, not random “ruin noise.”
- Light: overcast coastal gray with occasional strong warm domestic windows; broken wet ground is a high-value readability challenge.
- Distinct encounter shape: long narrow ferry ramps, open terraced courtyards and gated sluices that teach line and footing mechanics.

**Keld — the scarred high ground**
- Massive rock planes, ordered quarry benches, timber-supported extraction shafts and carved haul marks.
- Tall vertical cliff edges frame an isometric view but do not block the character; use fade/occlusion.
- Stone rubble sizes show operation: chiselled blocks near work benches, huge blasted slabs only where a believable collapse occurred.
- Light: dark stone, muted kiln amber, cold daylight at exposed faces, pale stress fractures as tiny cues.
- Distinct encounter shape: switchback paths and galleries with fixed machinery/collars, controlled choke points.

**Verran — wealth is a maintained elevation**
- City built *up* a ridge: upper stone-built storage and counting halls, lower reclaimed port with uneven thresholds and repeated repointing.
- Grain silos, rope, iron ties and ledger plaques instead of generic imperial banners.
- Narrative art target: the protected lower harbor looks worth preserving. If it resembles a villain fortress, the moral tension fails.
- Light: window clusters and organized civic lamps contrasted with shadowed, overworked docks; crisp lines that gradually deviate by the waterfront.
- Distinct encounter shape: layered plazas, narrow record corridors, exposed loading decks and public measurement boards.

**Selwick — the outer force**
- Heavy sea-facing polygonal stone, rusted bracing girders and oversized but mechanically traceable piston frames.
- Exposed seawall has wet wind abrasion and salt; inside, high-contrast engineering silhouettes guide phase reads.
- Each major machine must have a believable **load path** (gear → collar → fitted stone → receiving/donor route). Don't build free-floating spiked monuments.
- Light: severe exterior sky and rare reflected surf white; interior shutters and practical lanterns, charged seams only under load.
- Distinct encounter shape: interleaved walkways, safe shoulders, repeating arcs of pressurized machinery.

**Anset / Sere — absence, not spectacle**
- Anset: exposed test foundations, calibration rings, a wrecked beacon; evidence of a public engineering decision.
- Sere: a few dry stone courses and market steps visible only at specific ebb. No giant intact haunted capital, no spectral population.
- Distinct image: tax inscriptions and door thresholds under seaweed are more unsettling than an army of skeletons.

## 5. Form language for professions and enemies

**Shorer:** hinge angle, thick tapered tool, planted support edges, shallow horizontal cracks; contact is broad and decisive. **Sounder:** plumb-line arc, paired rods, strict tick marks around real enemy motion; precise narrow releases. **Linewright:** visible cords and weighted hooks, three-node geometry, moving reeling stance and thin taut lines with clear physical start/end.

Class silhouette identifiers must read on black material in **0.3-second thumbnail recognition tests**; no reliance on helmet colors or full-screen VFX. Profession armor is protective workwear evolved into combat equipment, not generic knight/wizard/ranger costumes.

**Bridleshells:** low forward armored volume, distinctly softer tail joint, scrapes and weight shift as tell. **Chalk colonies:** fine pale branching surface patterns and clustered mineral forms that appear to breathe only when loaded. **Held:** readable damaged mechanical harness around a **recognizable person**, not a zombie. **Service crews:** working uniforms differentiated by tools, insignia and behavior, not black-red villain armor.

## 6. Visual storytelling shots (concept briefs, not commissioned art yet)

**Keyframe K1 — Gauge Steps:** camera high above diagonal stone steps; official tide post fixed on wall while privately carved lines lie far below; Tessa leans against a rope bundle counting passenger slots. *Story information visible before dialogue:* something has moved that should not have moved.

**Keyframe K2 — North Face, Keld:** a casting die sits beside its new identical impression; opposite walls show white stress cracks beneath repaired supports; bright warm kiln light only at the edge. *Story information:* the technology came from skill and labor, and evidence persists in matter.

**Keyframe K3 — Sere at low water:** a former market corner barely clears the tidal mud; Ada is knee-deep beside a tax mark, not posed atop a monumental ruin. The horizon is mostly water. *Story information:* an inhabited place was administratively removed, not mythically vanished.

**Keyframe K4 — Sixth Jack:** numbered frames across a six-jack working array; the reserve sixth jack is the active runaway machine, with a broad central piston and two secondary load paths in view, the arena floor giving way in **legible staged sectors**; thin white stress lines and one amber control station. *Story information:* the machine is dangerous but understandable. The opponent is a system, not a demon wearing industrial skin.

## 7. Interface — information as instrumentation

An original UI language taken from survey slates, calibration collars and stamped work orders, not a recreation of any recognizable ARPG glass or ornament.

- Health: strong horizontal/bar form with legible value and damage feedback; no iconic pair of giant red/blue spheres.
- Profession resource: **Shorer** loaded gauge with stress notches; **Sounder** compact ticks near target with clear expiry; **Linewright** clear node/edge count and tension bar linked to lines.
- Map: topographic/sounding-chart treatment with elevation bands, route closures and clear accessible icons. Decorative stippling never impairs wayfinding.
- Inventory: tactile fitted component illustration, provenance and **what action changes** before small stat math. Avoid microscopic text.
- Quest evidence: a simple **work folio** with observation, source, claimed explanation and contradiction; the Third Column can be seen as a missing/covered ledger field early, not a cheap red string wall.
- Accessibility: text scaling, contrast modes, UI navigation without mouse, motion/flash/shake controls and captions for environmental events.

**UI risk:** engineering-themed microtext and hairlines can look attractive but fail at game speed. Build layout in native rendered resolution early; test at 1366×768, 1920×1080, zoomed browser and controller.

## 8. Lighting, weather, materials and effects

**Rendering hypothesis:** physically credible roughness and dampness, restrained bloom, selective cold rim lighting, strong floor separation. On low settings, simplify reflections, not cues. Weather builds atmosphere without writing hostile attack tells out of the image.

**Materials:** layered stone, grain directions in timber, corrosion around actual joins, limited polished metal on tools. Avoid every surface equally glossy. Seamglass has unique fracture-reflection response; never neon blue crystal scattered as generic loot.

**Effect budgets:** each move uses a short grammar of anticipation/contact/falloff. Enemy warning layers outrank class spectacle. Small particles communicate material (stone chips, chalk dust, rope fibers), not unlimited orange sparks. Do not use saturated full-screen explosions for routine hits.

## 9. Audio pillars and authored silence

**Sound categories:**
- **World bed:** changing surf exposure, far rope taps, rain against waxed cloth, tide bells and hollow quay resonance; soundscape has real geography.
- **Mechanical truth:** collar tick before a load, a double ratchet before a piston stroke, seam fracture hiss, brace foot scrape; sounds reinforce physical cause.
- **Combat identity:** Shorer = deep sprung metal/ground return; Sounder = tight paired calibration pings + plumb impact; Linewright = fiber tension, spool whirl and clean snap.
- **Fauna:** shells and mineral grit, not demonic growls by default. Held retain muffled human labor noises where appropriate.
- **Music:** restrained low strings/skin percussion/metal resonances built from thematic rhythms; heroic melody or choir earned sparingly. No “dark fantasy drone on every screen.”

**Silence as a scene beat:** the sound of a gauge stair settling while no sea wave hits it; Sere's low-water landing with only shoes and rope; the breath before the Sixth Jack re-engages. Accessibility captions must preserve vital cues without requiring sound.

**Mix priority:** player impact/evade, imminent hostile hit, class tool state, critical spoken line, environmental texture, music. Any repeated call or chorus must survive prolonged combat sessions without exhausting the listener.

## 10. Grounded visual-study references — not asset sources

- Tidal-island access and exposed flats: [ESA's Mont Saint-Michel coastal imagery/context](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/earth-observation-programmes/earth-from-space-tidal-island). Learn **how low tide exposes channels**, not abbey architecture to imitate.
- Stone-cut and causeway erosion: study real maritime retaining walls, old port gauges and quarry working methods. Commission or create wholly original concept material; do not import reference photographs or other games' concept art into shipped assets without rights.
- Style comparison: the **readability and material finish** of premium isometric ARPGs is a bar, **not** a template for copying their UI silhouettes, costumes, iconography or VFX timings.

**Imagery to explore next:** the four keyframes above, a recognizable silhouette for each worker profession, early enemy drawings, and a combat HUD that keeps information clear against dark stone. The images should make Alasia more concrete and playable, rather than merely decorative.
