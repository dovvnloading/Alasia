# Atlas, routes, settlements and economies

**ID:** WRL-002 · **Status:** WORKING · **Depends on:** WRL-001 · **Version:** 0.1

The world begins with **one interdependent basin**, not a continent filled with unrelated biomes. These are *relative* positions; final distance, coastline and elevation require a geographic blockout.

## 1. Spatial model — the Inner Sound

| Location | Relative position | Elevation / physical role | Travel / dependence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Keld** | Northwest | High, fractured quarry plateau | Stone and fitted seamglass depart by sheltered freight route toward Verran and Orren |
| **Verran** | North-central | Stable ridge above vulnerable reclaimed harbor | Stores imported Orren grain; allocates lift; receives the main trunk from Selwick |
| **Orren** | Southwest | Low alluvial terraces on donor-side shelf | Feeds and crews the Sound; ferries connect its quays to Verran and Keld |
| **Selwick** | East, at oceanward throat | Narrow barrier and built stormwall | Controls the outer surge, tide-driven power and the main sill trunk |
| **Anset** | East-southeast of the Inner Sound | Old test shoal, usually underwater | Warning beacons and exposed foundations accessible at exceptional low water |
| **Sere** | South of Orren, on connected low shelf | Former inhabited donor, now submerged | Historic boat route and ruined approaches; salvage only under narrow tidal windows |
| **Outer Bar** | Beyond Selwick east | Breaking shoal | Makes an open-ocean escape and direct external trade difficult |

**Connectivity:** Keld–Verran freight, Keld–Orren maintenance vessels, Verran–Orren staple trade, Verran–Selwick regulated service route, Orren–Sere former ferry route, Selwick–Anset low-water inspection. Most crossings are by **boat**; there are no implausible trans-island roads. Short causeways connect quay wards to local tidal facilities, not one island to another.

**Weather logic:** ocean swell and severe weather enter from the eastern throat. Selwick's barrier limits surge in the Inner Sound. High Keld terrain has steeper runoff, while Orren absorbs river silt, which explains their distinct material and agriculture. Storm exposure and ebb access shape when quests can happen.

**Distance discipline:** travel takes a meaningful part of a day and depends on weather, tide and ferry availability; exact kilometers and minutes remain **TBD pending a scale map**. The campaign compresses travel via authored transitions rather than pretending these islands are adjacent neighborhoods. Loading zones represent ferries, safe paths and time skips honestly.

## 2. Verran — the receiver, not an evil castle

**Why here:** a northern ridge above navigable inner harbors makes a logical administrative port. The protected lower docklands are not naturally high ground; without controlled lift they flood frequently. The Board of Sills governs from here because every island depends on the harbor's receipts, imported goods, dry warehouses and trained surveyors.

**Economy:** bonded warehouses, grain storage, charts, rope making, drydocks, salt-tax and freight insurance. It imports stone, fitted parts, fish and grain. Its influence comes from **route leverage**, not unexplained legions.

**City fabric:** tall stepped granaries on masonry plinths, contiguous narrow streets uphill, replacement tide marks under fresh render, iron gauge gates at the lower docks. The upper ridge is populous and securely above the sea; the lower port is where the allocation stakes are visible.

**Politics and contradictions:** Verran's workers need the same food as Orren's; not everyone benefits equally from the Board. A dock porter may support the grain lifeline and still oppose the concealment. Public protests can cut access to the very pier needed for evacuation.

**Scenes with function:** **Counting Hall** (Board decisions and archive access); **North Granary** (demonstration of material stakes and supply-chain dependency); **Lower Dock Steps** (visible historic lift marks). Each supports a story beat and a playable route.

**Removed and what breaks:** without Verran, there is no plausible reason why officials would sacrifice the distant donors: the central receiving harbor and regional food bottleneck are the incentive.

## 3. Keld — the supplier with a real bargaining position

**Why here:** the high fractured plateau exposes deep seams and yields workable granite. Unlike the low donor islands, its settled farms are scarce but its quarry benches remain habitable above dangerous tides.

**Economy:** blockstone, seamglass extraction and calibration, braceworks, iron fittings. Imports staple grain, timber and medicine. Their **Stonewrights** know that replacement collars are becoming harder to make; some concealed fractures to keep wages and food shipments flowing.

**Social fault:** younger workers demand a full accounting of where fitted collars go. Older crews know that stopping shipments could endanger their relatives on Orren. The tension is material and generational, not “mine dwarves versus court nobles.”

**Built form:** stacked spoil ramps, counterweight lifts, washhouses, wedged retaining walls and mine galleries in which strain makes a recognizable ticking sound. Warm kilns in cold rain; white fracture seams on otherwise dark stone.

**Scenes:** **North Face** (excavation where obsolete and new routing stamp marks overlap); **Old Kiln** (source of historical casting dies and metallurgy); hillside labor kitchens (why production can't simply stop). No mystical cavern added solely for a color palette.

**Removed and what breaks:** the setting loses the source of its technology and the labor history behind the Board's apparent miracles.

## 4. Orren — the first island at risk

**Why here:** mud-rich alluvial ground and protected inlets support market farms, reed fields, ferries and repair yards. It is also the easiest new donor because it lies on the **same low connected shelf as Sere**, and its foundations are less resistant than the ridge islands.

**Economy:** grain, beans, reed rope, shallow-draft barges, salted river fish. The Orren Boat League keeps a shared ledger of rescue vessel capacity, an uncomfortable practical answer to the Board's evacuation promises.

**Built form:** raised floors on patched stone feet, drainage cuts, woven windbreaks, ferry ramps, water marks cut by families rather than state surveyors. Walls reveal old repairs beneath new repairs. No city-scale cathedral ruin on a tiny farming island.

**First vertical-slice district (distinct playable functions):**
- **Low Quay:** safe hub turned crowded embarkation point; grounded characters, trade, evacuation priorities.
- **Gauge Steps:** official and private tide marks do not agree; the first visible discrepancy and combat teachable space.
- **Reedward:** fields and terraced cottages cut by newly flooded ditches; civilians and displaced fauna.
- **South Cut:** maintenance sluice and illegally opened donor valve; multi-stage combat encounter and first incontrovertible work order.

**Structural arc:** water displaces fauna → the player clears a real route → a faulty collar reveals intentional donor routing → saving a group consumes boat time needed elsewhere → the evidence identifies a handoff to higher authorities.

**Removed and what breaks:** the player lacks a lived, inhabited first consequence; the central political accusation would remain abstract.

## 5. Selwick — the necessary control point

**Why here:** a narrow island at the Sound's eastern mouth constricts tidal flow. The engineers can harvest surge with mills and protect the inner islands behind an expensive stormwall. This makes it the only plausible home for the largest pressure-routing assembly. The **Outer Sill** has six numbered load jacks: five form the normal working array, and the sixth is a reserve/overflow shunt. Repeated misuse of that sixth unit explains its overload at the campaign climax. Its number is stamped on service plates; it is not a legendary title.

**Economy:** tide machinery, pilotage, salvage, stormwall maintenance, lighthouse service. Food imports mean crew loyalties are mixed; closing the main trunk can save one shore and ruin the port workers' own homes.

**Built form:** seawall in thick polygonal stone, bronze collar housings, iron race doors, low beacon towers, wet plank walkways on the exposed face. Workshop light is practical; there are no arbitrary infernal furnaces.

**Scenes:** **Breach Walk** (evacuation and windswept traversal); **Outer Sill** (the main routing system, controllable but structurally unstable). The final encounter is machinery under pressure and organized opposition, not an unrelated cosmic monarch.

**Removed and what breaks:** the physical means to make a final allocation is missing, and the player has no place where the last decision can be enacted.

## 6. Two lost places, each with a different purpose

**Anset — the accepted test.** A small, mostly uninhabited shoal with experimental stone rings and a former warning beacon. Public engineering accounts and visible foundation plates establish that the ancient trade-off was once transparent. Its submersion demonstrates the system's capacity limit. **Not** another hidden massacre, not a full dungeon city.

**Sere — the denied cost.** Forty-one years ago Sere had lived-in quay steps, property parcels, public burial marks and registered fishing families. The Board rerouted settlement there after a winter storm damaged Verran's harbor defenses. Inundation and rapid subsidence destroyed homes; survivors carried documents and traditions to Orren and Verran. At extreme low water, only portions of the **Market Steps** and workshop foundations emerge. The truth is discovered through material evidence and living testimony, never a spooky ghost flashback.

**Distinct storytelling roles:** Anset proves the rule. Sere proves the abuse. Orren proves that the abuse continues. **No third lost island unless a new causal role passes review.**

## 7. Societies and institutions — friction without factions of cardboard

**Board of Sills:** chartered governors of routing decisions, historically justified as public emergency coordination. Authority derives from freight agreements and control of critical harbors. Their failure is secrecy, coerced risk distribution, and preservation of privilege.

**Office of Soundings:** gauge-keepers and chart surveyors; a few were ordered to hide donor allocations, while many field hands suspect manipulation. Divided loyalties make it a good site for believable defections.

**Orren Boat League:** ferrymen, pilots and freight boat owners with a strict capacity ledger. Able to rescue people but not indefinitely; they demand decisions with names and numbers.

**Keld Stonewrights:** craft/trade association that controls safely fitting seamglass. Expert labor is scarce and not replaced by a magical factory. They have leverage but no food independence.

**Selwick repair crews:** organized within the sill service, not a separate evil cult. Some will attack intruders because reckless tinkering can drown the Sound. Their orders and personal motives must be visible.

## 8. Daily life and cultural specificity

- Names and measurements are recorded on **shore posts** placed at known levels. They're civic markers first, memorials later; replacing one is a public argument about what counts as land.
- Marriage, property, shipping and burial depend on where “dry ground” legally begins. Sere families can have intact deeds and no recognized island.
- Tide bells announce safe passage and scheduled valve work; they are job instructions, not mystic prophecy.
- Wet-weather clothing is layered waxed cloth, leather and repaired iron fittings, with class-profession harnesses identified by load scars.
- Residents know when floodwater is normal; a silent drop in land while the tide remains on schedule is terrifying precisely because it is **abnormal**.
- Local folklore can be contradictory, but physical laws remain consistent. A fisher may call a cracking seamglass wall “angry”; the world does not certify that the stone has a god inside it.

## 9. Growing the atlas

Further locations should emerge from the routes, stone, food and choices already established here. We don't need another island simply to introduce another biome. A new place is worthwhile when it changes people's lives, the campaign, or the ways the player moves and fights.
