# Three landings in the Inner Sound

At Keld, a freight boat ties up bow and stern under the tackle at the foot of the quarry ramp. At Verran a loaded ferry lies so low against the grain berth that from the queue you can watch a sailor's cap going about on its own. At Selwick the service pilot keeps the wall between her passengers and the outer sea. Passengers step out into still air. At the bend in the wall they can see white water climbing the outer face, level with the lintel of the door they just came through.

## Keld · The kitchen on the turn

The quarry road doubles back above the landing. Stone takes the long outside of the bend on a drag. People go straight up a stair cut through its middle. The kitchen stands in the crook of the road, its pantry door on the cart side and its serving hatch on the stair. You can step out of the pantry on the flat with a heavy sack and stand at the hatch with the masthead of your boat below your feet.

The woman upstairs grows onions in the trough of a broken grindstone. When the rain runs brown over the lip she goes out with a dustpan and saves what she can from the step below. The cook buys Orren beans dry enough to rattle and soaks them the evening before a burn. In the morning the fitters take bowls uphill and warm their flat cakes on the kiln ledge. On her sister's birthday the cook leaves an onion whole in the pot. Her sister fishes it out by the tail.

There is a show in the cupboard under the upper road. Its door opens into the dim kitchen. In place of the back panel someone has stretched a square of worn sailcloth. On the pantry side Nis, a piece hand at North Face, works the cutouts by a lamp. Near the wick the shadow of her bird grows big enough to swallow the stool painted on the cloth. Every child on the stair knows what's coming next. There is a setter who always wipes the seat of his stool with the back of his cap. For several shows now the bird has been looking for somewhere clean enough to lay.

**First high water.** On the way up to North Face the cupboard is shut. Come down again after the foreman has stopped the gallery line for the worker. The kitchen is full of piece hands who still have half a shift in them. At the hatch the setter wants the cook to give him supper on credit. She still owes the flour carrier for breakfast. Behind him the bird examines the painted stool and wipes it with the back of one wing. The setter turns to see what's so funny. He holds his cap under it. A wooden egg bumps down a fold in the cloth and lands in the cap. He takes it back to his daughter. His bowl is cooling at the hatch. If the worker shortened the stoppage in [Cold Fittings](../02-narrative/writing-and-quest-standards.md#supporting-quests), the setter takes his bowl back uphill to finish the piece he has on the bench. Otherwise the cook pushes the bowl across and lets his tab run another night.

## Verran · A room with two sleepers

From the ridge the lower grain berth and Washhouse Steps look close enough for a porter to step from one to the other. At the bottom a boatbuilder's wall divides the deep berth from the strip of sloping shore. A porter has to go up and over along the ridge stair. At the Steps a crew eases a boat alongside and pushes wet gear across the sill into the washroom. Over the wall a hoist can lift a sling of grain clear of a deep hold. Lose the sloping landing and even a wet coat has to go the long way round on somebody's back.

Fresh roof water feeds the washer's covered tank. Salt dries in pale half-moons where the sleeves of the boat coats bend. The washer turns the sleeves over the end of a board to clean out the folds. There is a spare coat on a peg for a crew hand who needs to go up the ridge while their own dries. It was made for a very tall person. Half the harbor has gone courting in it with the hem tucked up. The washer charges nothing if you come back with news of how it went.

Two workers rent a little room off the lower washroom in opposite watches. Moss works nights on the hoist. Rin takes the early launch out with the harbor pilots. Each gets the bed to themselves. Between them they pay the owner rather more than a room ordinarily brings. They share a pair of slippers. Moss leaves them pointing into the room for Rin. She leaves them pointing at each other because she thinks it looks like the slippers are having a talk.

**Third and fourth highs.** After the hearing, the heavy ferry is still tied up and Moss has been stood down. Rin's launch still runs. She comes in to sleep and finds Moss sitting on the bed in his good shirt and their slippers. He takes them off. She gives him one back. They sit there with one bare foot apiece. On the fourth watch the washer offers the room over her brother's shop. It gets the sun but a day lodger already sleeps there. The relief crew has one looking onto a blank wall, dry and theirs for both shifts. While Moss tries the corner of the stair with a rolled mattress, Rin tries the key. It turns. She asks for a second one. They can't pass it hand to hand while she's out on a boat. The crew brings back two keys and sets aside rent from Verran's freight-dues deposit. Moss and Rin each get a copy of the terms and each can still decline. If either does, the mattress can go back downstairs. At Selwick the watch will have the standing order for phased release. The player can take the heavy end on either trip.

## Selwick · Enough floor for a winter net

In the lee of the wall, sprats rise after the sheltered water has gone still. From the top walk they first show as a patch where the reflection of the wall breaks up. Then a dark back goes through the middle, the shoal turns, and for an instant the underside of the overhang lights up with reflected silver. The Net Yard's inside door stands open on that water. A mender who has spent her whole day looking down can tell when it happens by the light passing over her hands.

The pilot whose son works Verran's grain hoist shares a winter net with her sister. The floats along the top are wooden, rubbed small at the waist where the cord goes round them. Each weight at the foot sits in a twine pocket like the toe of a stocking. Rolled up wet it fits in the belly of a small boat and takes two to lift. Open it on land and it reaches from the inside door clear across the apron. The sisters set the far edge on the little hooks so the daylight shows through. Follow a crooked row of diamonds along the net and your finger comes out through a tear. Their father sits on the low sill by the hooks. One daughter walks the edge through his hands, the other works the far side. He can mend sitting there with his shoes off.

The family sells much of its catch fresh on the service landing. The father takes fish for his work, cleans them at home and fries the little backbones crisp enough to eat with the fingers. His grandson used to go straight past the fillets and take the bones. The old man thinks they're teaching the boy bad habits in Verran, where they discard the best part of a fish.

**Fifth high water.** While the apron is still dry the pilot gives the worker one end of the net and walks away with the other. Walk the float edge towards the inside door. It comes straight just as the worker runs out of yard. Then the pilot unhooks her end and feeds the same net through the double doors of a shoreward shed above the apron, offered as a place to keep the gear. The rope fits easily. Almost half the web lies folded over itself in the doorway. They can inspect it in lengths in there, laying each stretch they've finished back onto wet mesh to see the next one. “We could do a piece on every watch,” says the pilot. Her sister drops the mending needle into her hand. “My watch, you mean. You'll be out on the boats.” At that the pilot comes into the shed, lays her good coat on the wet pile to sit on, and takes the first row herself. Putting the gear away gives the family its floor back for the meeting.

After a shared setting, show the net leaving by boat in its familiar compact bundle. It will come open on somebody else's floor. The daughters have to decide whether to take their father on the boat as well. Going out to work used to mean opening his front door. Under phased release he keeps his place at the hooks. His grandson has put in for a shift unloading grain by hand. On the homeward service boat the pilot seeks out the worker. If the net has gone, she wants a word sent back from Verran about a mending place where her father could sit. If it's on the hooks, she's wrapped up a paper twist of fried bones for her son's break.
