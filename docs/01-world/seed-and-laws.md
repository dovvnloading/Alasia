# World seed and the physical laws of Alasia

**ID:** WRL-001 · **Status:** WORKING · **Dependencies:** FND-001, GOV-002 · **Version:** 0.1

## 1. The event that creates the world

The Inner Sound is an archipelago of faulted stone plates separated by shallow tidal channels. Its low-lying settlements survived for generations through masonry, tide gates, drainage cuts and a rare material found in deep fractures: **seamglass**. When a fitted piece of seamglass is driven by tidal power, it can store a deformation and redirect strain through a connected seam in the bedrock.

Early engineers used the phenomenon to correct a wall settling on one side of a harbor. Later, larger **sills** raised critical foundations by small but lasting increments. The cost became clear: **a receiving plate gains lift only while another connected plate suffers settlement**. The physical work comes from the tide machinery; the material cannot create ground from nothing. Routing is lossy, hazardous and finite.

Anset, a mostly uninhabited low shoal, was the first publicly accepted donor. In a winter emergency forty-one years before the campaign, authorities deliberately redirected the donor side to **inhabited Sere** to keep Verran's grain harbor usable. Sere's lower ground sank faster than its people could evacuate. The records of the donor choice were concealed, and the official account called Sere's loss an exceptional storm disaster. The people affected were never universally deceived; their testimony was discounted.

Now Anset's foundation is exhausted, Sere cannot bear further settlement, and the network is quietly drawing from **Orren**. It begins to fail on the day we meet the player.

## 2. Rule set — these cannot be hand-waved

**R-01 — Strain, not spellcraft.** Seamglass is a fantasy mineral whose unusual property is the retention and transmission of *mechanical strain*. It does not summon electricity, create matter, resurrect people, change minds or stop time. Its effects need contact or a constructed conductive route: fitted stone seams, metal collars, or sufficiently short field cables and tools.

**R-02 — The account must balance.** Any sustained elevation benefit has a donor that settles. The elevations are **not** one-to-one in meters because areas, strata, losses and geometry differ; there is nevertheless a real, measurable displaced volume and irreversible damage. Every permanent allocation has a nameable recipient and donor. The tide supplies work to operate the sills, but work does not remove the donor obligation.

**R-03 — Distance has a price.** Load routes follow connected plates, buried fittings and metal conductors. Deep open water interrupts the system without expensive anchored links. A handheld implement can work across a short battlefield, **not** across an island. The main trunks are enormous fixed sites. The line of sight and collision rules for classes derive from this.

**R-04 — Capacity is finite.** Seamglass develops chalk-white stress veins; beyond its rated capacity it fractures. Braces, collars, cables and human bodies fail before a region can carry arbitrary load. Field implements store only brief impulses. Large sills need tide-driven gearing and constant maintenance. No perpetual ultimate ability.

**R-05 — Change is not free in either direction.** Turning a sill off does not instantly undo decades of settlement. A sudden interruption may remove support from a receiving plate, and a sudden reroute can tear buildings or release stored strain into workers. Restoration is construction and relocation, not a one-scene purification.

**R-06 — There is no perfect secrecy.** Gauges, stone marks, floods and work orders reveal what has happened. Eran Velt and the Board can alter **public registries**, survey access and the language of culpability; they cannot remove memory from people or erase geological evidence. Civilian witnesses disagree on motives, not on whether they once had homes.

**R-07 — Biological effects are material.** Animals sense repeated pulses; some shelter in energized fractures. Chalk colonies feed on minerals and are mechanically stimulated by cycling strain. Injured workers may remain entangled in faulty harnesses. They are not demon-possessed and there is no universal necromantic plague.

## 3. Scale discipline: two implementations of one law

| Scale | Application | Source | Cost / cap | Playable consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Civil engineering | Elevate a foundation or gate approach over years | Tidal gearing, fixed seamglass collars | Donor settlement, work crews, geologic limits | Coastal politics, timed access, disasters |
| Workshop | Bend a plate, move a jack or tune a brace | Flywheels, hand cranks, fixed fittings | Material fatigue, local load | Interactive props and crafting provenance |
| Field profession | Hold, read, route or release a brief impulse | Impacts, tensioned tools, local vibration, carried reserves | Short reach, recovery time, overload risk | Classes with distinct resources and skill timing |

**No false equivalence:** a Shorer's attack is **not** literally sinking an island. It manipulates the same material response at a vastly smaller scale. Major terrain changes require fixed sills and cannot be done with a combat ultimate. A Sounder's resonant shock comes from stored or incoming force in a nearby conductive fitting, not a magical sound beam passing through arbitrary stone.

## 4. History must follow the rules

The official chart of the Inner Sound displays **observed level** and **scheduled lift**. The original engineering ledger also contained **donor settlement**: the suppressed **Third Column**. The public was told that uninhabited Anset was still absorbing the work, long after the routing had moved to Sere. That lie required a small conspiracy: the Board members with allocation authority, a fraction of survey leadership, and work crews compelled by safety orders. **Not** every civilian, engineer or guard is complicit.

Surviving evidence:
- Two generations of tide lines scratched into the same Orren steps, indicating land movement rather than sea rise.
- A routing collar stamped for Sere under an ostensibly Anset-only drawing.
- A Sere tax and property register that was never legally reconciled with the shipping map.
- Sill logs showing that Verran gained lift on the exact nights Sere lost ground.
- Testimony from those evacuated and those who refused to leave.

The story's mystery is not “what occult creature did it?” It is **who signed what, with whose knowledge, when another alternative existed, and what the living can now do**.

## 5. Material ecology and violence

A failure at a sill spreads a chain of effects: fracture and rapid ground movement → flooded burrows and ruptured storage caverns → displaced **Bridleshells** and pulse-fed **chalk colonies** → attacks in narrow evacuation corridors → contract crews contesting repair controls → injured, overloaded **Held** who must be restrained or their harnesses carefully disabled.

This supports ARPG combat density without requiring the population to inexplicably turn into monsters overnight. Most hazards are concentrated near *work sites, flooded cuts, abandoned foundations and active conduits*. Normal fields, houses and markets should not be endless combat arenas.

Enemy taxonomy and ethical handling: [enemies, loot and progression](../03-gameplay/enemies-loot-and-progression.md). Creature behavior must follow vibration, territory or feeding logic rather than omniscient aggro.

## 6. Hard questions answered

**Why would sane people build it?** The Inner Sound regularly suffered inundation. The initial Anset tests relieved real harbor failures; safer trade brought grain and medicine. At first, uninhabited donor ground made the tradeoff seem acceptable.

**Why accept the Board's explanation?** Its published charts were technically accurate about high tide and protected port levels; donor records were missing. Observing two linked islands' relative movement needs access, expertise and reliable time series, which the Board controlled. Families from Sere did protest. The player finds suppressed *proof*, not a first-ever suspicion.

**Why doesn't the player smash every machine?** Emergency shutdown causes abrupt support loss at gates, bridges and grain docks; drowning can accelerate. The player first attempts containment, then develops an informed allocation alternative. The ending is not a giant red button.

**Why do people still live here?** Keld's high stone is secure; the Sound supplies sheltered fishing and trade; Orren feeds the city; local ancestry and property matter; travel to an unknown coast is dangerous and expensive. Residents organize, rather than waiting to be rescued by a hero.

**Why can one hero matter?** Their profession permits work in collapsing cuts before armies, officials and crowds can safely enter. They uncover specific evidence, protect corridors and operate equipment. They cannot personally evacuate thousands or rebuild the shelf. **Other people do indispensable work.**

**Why is this dark fantasy and not industrial engineering fiction?** Seamglass and its scale behavior are speculative, non-scientific material laws; pressure-born creatures and large mechanical set pieces are uncanny. But the uncanny has rigorous limits, and the people live in a medieval-to-early-mechanistic material culture rather than a modern technical society.

## 7. Invariants for all downstream docs

- A donor island is **never** a mana battery that can be reused after its rock is exhausted.
- Published maps can omit Sere; a sailor or survivor can still know it existed.
- No class teleports, rewinds events, raises the dead, summons armies or generates limitless lift.
- The tide alone does not explain Orren's overnight drop; a specific **routing failure** and evidence must.
- The climax must show why both continuing and stopping the existing allocation can harm people.
- Sere's dead and displaced are not just quest tokens; their property, records and unresolved claims matter.
- A critical world event must be witnessed or inferred in gameplay, not deposited only in a codex.

## 8. Unresolved material-science questions (P0/P1 for prototype)

**P0:** On-screen rule for how field tools receive and spend force, visually perceivable without a lecture. **P0:** Conservative simulation model for local terrain interaction, independent of real-world tectonics. **P1:** Measured relative elevations and tide amplitudes for specific harbors, after atlas graybox. **P1:** Exact compression/settlement relationship and what a failed collar looks/sounds like. **P1:** Whether seamglass deposits naturally regrow (default: **no**, making mining political). **P1:** Relationship between recurring chalk-colony blooms and industrial overuse, validated through creature design.

Use only conservative, testable game rules until these are decided. Do not fabricate scientific numbers for flavor.
