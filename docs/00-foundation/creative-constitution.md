# The heart of Alasia

**Creative foundation · Working direction**

## Where this world begins

Alasia did not begin as a map with empty regions to fill. It began with a single discovery: **protecting one inhabited shore causes another to settle**. The rare fitted material known as seamglass can route strain through prepared stone. Large tidal machines keep low harbors usable; they cannot create land or abolish their cost. Over generations, a real achievement in civil engineering became the basis of an unequal political arrangement.

The game's central question is not simply who built the machines. It is **who was permitted to choose which shore paid**.

Early engineers openly sacrificed an almost empty shoal, Anset. In a later emergency the Board of Sills used the inhabited island of Sere to save the grain harbor at Verran, concealed the transfer, and denied the displaced families proper redress. The burden subsequently moved to Orren. People remember Sere; the government's charts just stopped admitting it. In the present, the transfer begins to break down.

The player is a field-worker whose profession allows entry into dangerous structures. Through fights, rescues, damaged instruments, working families and conflicting records, they understand what the islands were built upon. No prophecy singles them out. Their expertise matters because it lets them **act**, but other people remain essential to any solution.

## The world must feel lived in

Verran is not an evil capital. Its port keeps other islands fed. Keld's quarries make the fittings and depend on imported food. Orren's low agricultural ground grows that food and is now bearing hidden settlement. Selwick's stormwall is the only viable place for the major tidework machinery. Anset shows the original public trade-off; Sere shows the choice authorities hid.

These locations exist in relation to one another. Boats, charts, property rights, quarry labor, tides and food shipments shape where people live and how they speak. An old repair scar or an unpaid harbor fee can carry more story than an invented royal dynasty.

The setting is dark without being monotonous. Repairs are ingenious, markets are noisy, people have humor and private loves, and a well-lit house matters because the rain and failing stone beyond it are so real. A place becomes unsettling when we understand what has been lost there.

## Characters are not mouthpieces

Tessa Rul counts passengers and distrusts a government's impossible assurances; she must eventually bargain with the same harbor that put her island at risk. Iven Marr understands exact measurements but made himself complicit by publishing incomplete ones. Ada Sen has spent her adult life proving that a home erased from a chart was inhabited. Eran Velt once made an emergency choice with genuine reasons, then used those reasons to avoid admitting the continuing harm.

They have different loyalties, blind spots, voices and work to do apart from the hero. The drama arises when people who are partly right must bear the cost of being wrong.

## Action is part of the fiction

The combat should have the speed, weight, density and responsiveness expected of a premium top-down ARPG. Its distinctive mechanics come from the same physical imagination as the world:

- A **Shorer** plants and moves braces, receives impact and returns accumulated load.
- A **Sounder** reads the timing of an opponent's committed motion and answers at a meaningful opening.
- A **Linewright** anchors live connections and turns movement into redirected force.

These are **working class concepts**, not promises that originality is already solved. They should play differently even as simple gray shapes. We can revise or replace anything that proves tedious, generic or visually confusing.

## Visual character

Wet masonry, bronze load collars, pale stress veins, salt marks, repaired stairs, ferries and dark working clothes give Alasia its imagery. Wonder is rare and physically specific. Mood cannot conceal enemies or combat cues. We aim for high craft in lighting, animation, sound and narrative without borrowing recognizable creature designs, UI layouts or iconography from other games.

The bar is ambitious because **the work itself** must be distinctive: coherent places, memorable people, meaningful combat, well-edited writing and an excellent playable experience. No approval checklist can substitute for that.
