# Hyper-critique and rejection register — taste is tested, not declared

**ID:** PRD-002 · **Status:** WORKING / adversarial creative review v0.1 · **Review date:** 2026-11-13

**Caution:** The first writer cannot certify their own work as “AAA.” This is an initial **hostile edit**, not an independent reader panel, an external playtest, a historical authenticity review or legal/trademark clearance. The point is to identify failures *before* canon, art, VO, or engineering lock.

## 1. Evaluation rubric for every proper noun, scene and mechanic

Give each candidate an evidence-backed 1–5 grade on:
- **Causality:** could this exist only because of this world and its specific history?
- **Specificity:** does it point to a definite object, job, place or action rather than a fantasy mood?
- **Human plausibility:** would working people actually speak/behave that way?
- **Distinctiveness:** is it separable from recognizable established game imagery, familiar genre archetypes and arbitrary fantasy-name generation?
- **Play/production meaning:** does it enable useful interaction, performance-conscious staging or a needed narrative choice?

**1–2:** reject/rebuild. **3:** working candidate with risks. **4:** strong proposal pending external test. **5:** reserved for validated, exceptional work. No candidate below is awarded 5 on self-review. A “4” in a draft is **not** a lock.

**Required hostile questions:** Is it a familiar trope with the label changed? Would a real character say it? Does it require the audience to ignore economics or logistics? Is the poetic quality doing the work of causality? Can it be removed with no consequence? Does it copy a recognizable competitor? Can a team actually build/animate/test it to quality?

**Name rejection #N-001 — “Splicer.”** Initially considered for the cable profession, **rejected** because it is strongly associated with BioShock's recurring “Splicer” enemy category. Even though the ordinary trade term is valid, the game's market-facing identity would inherit a competitor association. [BioShock enemy reference](https://bioshock.fandom.com/wiki/Splicer). **Replacement in working docs: Linewright**, whose occupational meaning better expresses authored connections. This is a taste/association decision, *not* a legal determination or claim of exclusive use; an unrelated contemporary [Linewright software product](https://linewright.app/) uses the name, so clearance remains required.

**Name rejection #N-002 — “Plateback.”** First candidate for armored tidal fauna. A separately published game already uses **Plateback** as a creature name. [Fenyora creature listing](https://fenyora.com/game/). The former Aion citation did **not** substantiate the specific “Plateback Neokin” reference and has been removed, rather than retained as an unverified claim. **Replacement in working docs: Bridleshell**, tied to paired bridle-like ridges in the shell. Still pending silhouette, pronunciation and originality review.

**Reject as naming *patterns*, not as recorded used proposals:** “Shadowbinder,” “Blood Tide,” “the Eternal King,” “the Drowned Court,” “Soulreaper,” randomized apostrophe names. They promise mood, not world-specific function. If a future concept needs a religious/supernatural term, earn it from society's actual practice.

**Originality caveat:** Preliminary searches are not exhaustive trademark or design-comparison checks. A title, class name or enemy label can pass this editorial pass and still need independent clearance. No finding here warrants a statement of exclusive rights.

## 6. Enemy, loot and visual audit

**Bridleshells:** biology makes the first mob credible. Risk: a reskinned crab pack. Force an unmistakable paired foreplate ridge/side-joint silhouette and locomotion tell, not merely bronze armor and bigger size.

**Chalk colonies:** strain response differentiates from undead. Risk: fungal plague with chalk color. Make them mineral-building interconnected organisms whose emergence follows physical fissures. Avoid spore/poison clichés.

**Held:** potential human tragedy with hard gameplay edges. Risk: disability and industrial injury portrayed as monsters with no care. Require disarm/rescue options in selected scenes and human identities after; combat should not force every player to kill workers for materials.

**Contract crews:** risk of justifying hours of fights against ordinary laborers. Use controlled small counts, warnings and alternatives. Opponent violence needs staging and restrictions.

**Loot:** named craftwork and tools fit the economy. Risk: an encyclopedia of boring parts with minimal dopamine; verify that upgrades actually change class verbs and drops are legible at speed. Avoid fauna dropping formal manufactured equipment.

**Dark look:** risk of derivative desaturated gothic fantasy. We need functional shore architecture, repair scars, tense lines and small human warm lights. Contrast and combat legibility are nonnegotiable. Genre-level isometric framing is not a license to clone a recognizable HUD, class silhouette, VFX palette or monster.

## 7. Narrative logic attacks that must be survived

1. **Why didn't Sere's residents reveal the plot immediately?** They *did* protest. Official maps omitted them; expertise, freight power and denied compensation prevented effective accountability. Show Sere-born people alive and organized in scenes, not a population magically gaslit into silence.
2. **Why did the original Board choose inhabited Sere at Y-41?** Grain-port emergency, Anset exhausted, physical connection and refused public rationing/evacuation alternatives. Eran shares responsibility with earlier superiors. This cannot reduce to “there literally was no other option.”
3. **If the Board lied for forty-one years, why can the player suddenly expose it?** Converging evidence now available from new Orren rupture, stamped collars, casting records, relocated claims and gauge divergence. The player combines proof and facilitates access; Ada and coworkers were doing the work before.
4. **Why not shut down the whole system?** Rapid decompression and protected seawall/harbor dependence make it physically deadly. Explicitly demonstrate early, not merely in end-game dialogue.
5. **If Anset is gone and Sere destroyed, how can Plan A distribute load?** New fitted routes and managed *limited* settlement at evacuated marginal areas, not reuse of destroyed capacity. This remains a **high-priority spatial/engineering proof question**.
6. **Why does the hero have the final say?** They don't alone. Public emergency participants settle on two feasible plans; the player makes the physical operation possible and chooses among authorized alternatives. Strengthen the civic scene.
7. **Where is the joy and variety?** Work, food and maritime culture must be lively; otherwise every character is a mouth for the same moral dilemma. Add light, texture and interpersonal conflicts not reducible to a policy debate.
8. **Does the second half become a paperwork tour?** Pair evidence with changing encounters, labor disputes, floods, precise danger and genuine human stakes. Lore is rewarded by changed access and skill use.

## 8. Technical and production critique

- Three.js/WebGL can deliver polish, **but not without strict draw-call, overdraw, memory, animation and load budgets**. Claiming browser-AAA from prose is invalid.
- One shared “strain” conceit across three classes may make them feel samey. Force blind tests with neutral art. If two classes feel too similar, replace one, regardless of sunk naming effort.
- The “final boss is a machine” is creatively defensible but high-risk for ARPG emotional payoff. Build a compelling multi-stage enemy + environmental fight with prior personal stakes, not a tedious switch puzzle.
- Historical realism is inspiration, not a shield against magical inconsistency. A speculative mineral needs consistent boundaries and clearly legible costs.
- Existing AGPL-3.0 file is observed; derivative art/source and licensing terms remain unresolved. Production does not import other games' assets or copyrighted moodboard images just to fill a demo.
- First-slice priority outranks an expanded atlas, lore religion, open multiplayer economy and endgame system. Scope creep is a **quality failure**, not ambition.

## 9. Next hostile review protocol (mandatory)

**Pass 1 — Cold-read narrative:** a writer unfamiliar with the project explains each world event's cause, who benefits, where they traveled and what proof exists. Record confusion without explaining it away.

**Pass 2 — Name audition:** native speakers and genre players read every place/person/class name aloud, match it to a silhouette/location, and flag genericness or associations. Validate geographic/logical consistency, language appropriateness and potential trademark conflicts separately.

**Pass 3 — Gameplay blind test:** show five minutes per class with gray models and no class labels. Ask players to describe the *verb*, their decision pressure and why a mistake happened. Require distinct answers, not compliments.

**Pass 4 — Genre silhouette test:** compare monochrome art/UI silhouettes to well-known ARPG screenshots for recognizable derivative shapes; revise where too close.

**Pass 5 — Production attack:** technical lead prices/benchmarks the encounter and presentation; narrative/level lead identifies unreachable story states; rights reviewer validates asset provenance.

Every failed item needs a **decision ID, owner, proposed rewrite, validation test and retest date**. A world bible must be editable, not a shrine to its first fluent draft.
