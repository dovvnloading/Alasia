# Someone in the sleeve

Optional encounters at South Cut and the Verran grain stair. Vara Pike was on the door at Verran when a repair rig caught two men. One came out alive. At dinner he sits on the grain stair and peels his egg in one piece to annoy the man beside him.

## Vara Pike · South Cut, Orren

Vara keeps the key to the service cage on a long cord. In the evening her daughter can tell whether her mother is home by reaching through the cat hole and feeling for the key on its peg. The morning the stair dropped, Vara saw her daughter aboard. She told her the worker had walked the whole line. Offer this scene once the worker has admitted sending the stamped stub up with its scope blank. Vara makes her own copy. When she shuts her book, a strip of red wool hangs out of it.

**Lift the cover.** The wool is sewn to the cuff of a canvas sleeve, pressed flat between the back pages. A brass eye at the wrist has worn into a long oval. On the facing page Vara has written down two men’s names. Beside each she drew the same little upturned hand.

> **Worker** — Did they wave to you?  
> **Vara** — That's what I said at the door. I saw red go up twice. The carriers wanted in. I told them the men were answering us.  
> **Worker** — How long?  
> **Vara** — I went and had a piss. That's how long I thought we had.

Two fingers of red wool are stitched to the wrist so a watcher can see each hand in the poor light of a gallery. Vara passes her rule through the brass eye in place of the old rig's wrist pin. Move the rule. The empty cuff lifts. From the other side of the desk the wool comes up like a hand. She turns the rule over to show the worker where the heel of a hand could once have pressed against the cloth. There is nothing to see from the far side.

> **Vara** — This one was Tev's. They gave me his coat to take up to his wife. I sat outside with my little knife and cut the cuff off. I thought she'd ask how I knew.

**“Did she ask?”** Vara shakes her head. “She put an arm in it. She went about getting cups down with that sleeve hanging off her. I had the missing bit in my pocket the whole time.”

**“Who was in the other sleeve?”** “Jory. Eats his dinner under the grain stair. If you do see him, ask him if anybody touched his hand. He'll know what I mean.” Listening to either answer leaves the other available. Vara lets the worker draw the empty cuff in their folio. The cloth stays between her pages.

**Second high, on the way to Ada.** Vara's daughter has the book open on the bench at Low Quay. She has made the little red fingers come up. From the foot of the bench, the player can see the child's own hand lying quite still beside them. Vara comes out with two hot bowls and tells her to clear a place. Her daughter asks how to tell if a man inside can hear you. Vara sets the bowls down. “I don't know what he heard.”

## Jory Vale · The grain stair, Verran

Find Jory after the third-high public reading, on the bottom step clear of the ration queue. He shifted sacks before the accident. Now he calls the turns in the hand-carry line. He has a mouthful of egg when the worker first approaches. He holds up a finger, swallows and finishes the story he was telling his neighbor.

**“Vara wants to know if anyone took your hand.”** If the worker passed Vara by, the same conversation opens when a young carrier asks Jory how he got out of the rig. Jory turns his hand palm up. The three middle fingers are curled in. “Nobody took this. I could feel my nails going in. Thought I was making a fist.”

> **Jory** — There were two of us. Tev kept worrying at a bit of onion with his tongue. Made a click every time he got it between his teeth. I had my face up against his. Hours I used to eat beside that man and I couldn't have told you he did it.  
> **Worker** — Could you hear the woman at the door?  
> **Jory** — Plain as you now. I heard her say both of us were waving. You mind what you say when a fellow can hear.  
> **Worker** — What did the carriers do?  
> **Jory** — When the fitters got the weight off, somebody came round where I could see his boots. He said, “Look at my buckle.” I did. He went round the other side of me and made me do it again. First decent question anybody asked me that day.

**“What about Tev?”** “I can tell you when the clicking stopped. I couldn't tell his wife the rest and I can't tell you.” Leave time for him to finish the egg. He lays the curl of eggshell on his neighbor’s knee.

At the head of the stair a fitter has set out an unpowered practice frame. New carriers will spend their meal break trying it before the next repair call. Jory takes the worker up to it. “I can still wear the thing. I would like, just once, to see it from the door.” The fitter has clipped red wool to both cuffs. Press either wrist bar. Two empty sleeves rise together. Under the bench the bars join at the same shaft. Jory asks where the worker wants to stand.

- **Put your own arm in the sleeve.** Jory takes the observer's place. A fitter rocks the empty tool cradle by hand. From the door Jory calls “Stop” before the second lift. He comes back in, sets the door open another few inches and walks out again. Now he can see the worker's eyes. “You look bloody silly in there,” he calls. “I suppose I did too.” When he comes back in he borrows the fitter’s chalk. On the next visit the outline of his boots is still at the door, one heel in the passage. A new hand puts her boots inside the outline. A coat on its peg blocks her view. She moves sideways and asks the man on the floor to look round at her.
- **Take the door and watch Jory.** The fitter rocks the cradle. The red goes up. Call for the fitter to stop, come inside and get down where Jory can see you. He deliberately keeps his eyes still. Wait beside him or call the second carrier over. If the worker stays, she comes over when she sees the line has stopped. When she kneels on his other side, Jory says, “There. Now if I can't answer, there's still somebody with me.” On the next visit two carriers rehearse this together. One has rolled up her good coat so the head of the man on the flags has somewhere to rest.

Walking on leaves Jory to finish his meal. The fitters can still teach the ordinary drill. Both conversations and the drill fit in their existing watches. The fourth-high hand-carry shift passes the practice frame on the way to Kit’s stair. Show the changed drill there while the powered hoist stands idle.

**Back on Orren after the setting.** Find Vara at the service desk above South Cut, folding her canvas to fit the book. If the worker spoke to Jory, she sets it down to listen. The player can tell her what Jory heard, or tell her about the carrier who got down to the floor with him. In the first case Vara asks the worker to stand at the door and say her exact words back to her. From the bench they sound perfectly clear. She fetches her pencil and writes *Jory heard me* under the two hands. In the second, she writes *He followed the buckle with his eyes*. On leaving, her daughter holds the door and Vara goes through, wearing the key round her neck. If nobody asked Jory, the page still has the two little hands. The player can sit there with her while she puts the sleeve away.
