# The Second Fit and the Effects Window

Two optional counters for the campaign. Visit the first on the climb to North Face and again when the gallery stops. The second opens off the Selwick service landing on the way to the Net Yard and on the walk back from the Sixth Jack. Each scene fits on the shore where the player finds it. Neither spends another high water.

## Keld · The Second Fit

“Lid, step or grave?” Tarn asks anyone who stops at the cold end of the Old Kiln court. He buys stone that already has two flat faces. A broken stair tread makes a fine bench top. A sink stone can usually give him two whetstones and something big enough to put a name on. For a grave he charges by the length of the inscription. He does the spaces free. That last part is painted on the sign in his sister Eve's hand. A customer on the stair can read it over the head of the one at the bench.

On the climb to North Face, find Eve coaxing sand out of a thin crack with a goose feather. The stone served forty years as the threshold of a Verran storehouse. When the storekeeper widened the door, a Keld stone boat took the old step as ballast on its way home. Tarn has a buyer for it as a shelf for her flour bins. He bows to the worker, opens a palm over his mouth and produces a tooth. It's a bit of white gravel. Eve holds out a saucer already half full of his teeth.

**Help turn the threshold.** The long edges have rubbed smooth. The underside is full of writing. Somebody cut names in tight rows, left a thumb of space, and started again from the top. With the goose feather Eve picks a little crescent of sand out of the last line. One row reads **Ossa, who sang**.

> **Worker** — Was she buried here?  
> **Eve** — I couldn't tell you where a single one is buried. They brought the names from Sere on paper. Mum kept the slips on that nail. She'd copy one, turn it face down on the nail and do the next. Father found a paper fish under the lot. A kid had cut it out to give her.  
> **Tarn** — He was all for chucking it. Went a whole summer with that fish in his hatband.

Ask **Who paid for the work?** Eve gives the stone a small push. It rocks on the counter. “Verran paid for a threshold. Our mother dressed it on both faces. The man who took delivery never saw the bottom.” There were families settling in rented rooms with no room for a marker and no money for dry burial ground. Some had copies of inscriptions they had left behind. Some brought a name remembered after the last boat left their street. For as long as the slab was in the yard, the mason took them all. Forty years on, the people who can still read their mother's cramped cutting are selling the stone a second time.

Ask **Who was Ossa?** Tarn puts the feather right where the letters stop. “Mam came home singing what the woman sang her. Three of us learned it. I could do you the tune.” He gives the first two notes, then has to start higher. Eve gives him a third. There's a bad rhyme coming which makes them both smile. They have lost the words.

The worn housing goes in his scrap bin. | On a short test run through a trough, the first splash interrupts the bare tool. With the packing fitted the stroke finishes. After dressing, wipe it clean. A bright streak runs the whole width. Exchange a badly hollowed stone for a smaller flat one straight away or keep the width and collect the original on the descent. |
| Letter a keeper | Bring a spare buckle with a flat back. Two characters fit without thinning the metal. One straight kiln nail pays for the cutting. | Eve draws the letters on first. Flip the loose buckle and pick the face that will show once it is fitted. Tarn's own carries a goose with its beak open, crowded into the width of two letters. |

Ask to turn the threshold with empty pockets. Tarn says, “You see me asking the stone for money?” He gives the worker the cool end to lift.

### What goes back to the kitchen

On the descent a piece hand has set a little lamp flush with the writing. She can pick out *Ossa* from halfway across the court. Tarn has offered to return the flour buyer's deposit. He has enough laid by. The cook has offered a place for it on the retaining wall by the hatch. Eve would sooner keep it where somebody can put a thumb on a name. Either way she is going to have to tell the buyer why her flour bins are still standing on the wet flags.

- **Take it down to the serving hatch.** Put the threshold across the pantry cart. From the stair the names look upside down. The flour buyer comes out in her apron and finds Tarn and the worker trying to turn the stone in midair. She gets them to set it against the retaining wall on cleats, within reach of the kitchen steps. When the worker passes again, the woman is measuring the empty cart bed. Two planks that wide will make her a shelf. She holds her hand out to Tarn for the deposit. He counts it into her palm. “And four nails,” she says. He gives her the nails.
- **Slide the stone into the bench cradle, letters up.** Eve pins a strip of paper over each row. Rub it lightly with lampblack and the letters come up white across the dark. Hands going down to the landing can carry these strips back to the families in Verran and Orren. Eve had meant to make a neat list of them all. Tarn asks her to rub *Ossa, who sang*, exactly as their mother spelled it. On the way out the worker passes a hand with that short strip tucked into the crown of his cap to keep it dry on the freight boat. The flour buyer has her deposit back. She's looking over the stack of old treads with Tarn.

Leave the pair to settle it and the slab stays in its cradle, face up. On the shortened stoppage in *Cold Fittings* there are still two hands available for either job. The rest go back up the road as the first names come clear. At the lamp the piece hand has worked down to the last row. In a gap between names somebody has cut a little fish. One fin is missing. Tarn can still remember the shape of the paper where the nail tore through it.

## Selwick · The Effects Window

A salt-stiff coat hangs by one finger of its glove from a peg outside the service landing door. Somebody tried to save time by stitching the glove to the sleeve. Len Cett once paid two people for that coat. One sold him the sleeve while the other was wearing the rest of it. He keeps it by the door because it still makes him laugh.

Len lends things that hands from a passing boat might need for one landing. On the upper rail he sells **released effects**. Crews bring a person's bag down from the wall. Len inventories it, advances the family the value of anything they release, and holds it through two service sailings in case they want to buy it back. He has paid for more borrowed berths to Verran than he has ever slept in. The lower rail is numbered inside the cupboard. Everything on it still belongs to somebody.

On the fifth high, see Len bent over a wide blue coat. The right pocket has a long, neatly stitched mouth. Inside, it is too shallow to take more than the ends of his fingers. Someone worked a second pocket into the lining underneath.

> **Len** — That's where his pay went. If somebody got fingers into the outside one he'd feel them through the cloth. My brother slept beautifully on boats.  
> **Worker** — You'd know his coat anywhere.  
> **Len** — I know the pocket. I put it there. Clothes change hands a great deal on this landing.  
> **Worker** — What's the number for?  
> **Len** — Fifty-eight. They cut the coat off a man they got out of a frame. I'll give it back to fifty-eight when he comes down from the sickroom.

Len's brother Gav has been missing from an earlier repair shift for three years. They never brought his things down. Len would like one thing of his to come back through that window. The coat is the nearest he has come. Lift the cloth. The outer cuff has a thumbhole far below the old elbow patch. For a good long while someone shorter than Gav has been wearing it with the sleeves rolled back. Len saw that stitching the night the coat arrived. He has been taking it down and putting it back ever since.

### What's for hire

| Order | Terms | What changes hands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carry sling | Leave one loose bronze buckle as a deposit. Return the sling for that exact buckle. | A wide canvas cradle with a shoulder strap. One extra bundle of ordinary salvage fits against the hip, occupying a functional fitting slot until the sling comes off. Empty it before returning it. Two square patches on the strap show where it can be let out over a winter coat. |
| Left-hand glove | One tanned plate of bridleshell hide for one glove, drawn from the released rail. Choose a size and inspect the lining before exchange. | A replaceable glove for a carrying hand, with the hook wear turned to the outside. At the window Len will show how it wears under a sling. Return a badly fitting glove in the same visit and try the next size. |
| Find an owner | Len asks for the bag number and a description of something *inside* it. No fee. | Try **Ask about fifty-eight** on the blue coat. Len lets the worker examine the hidden pocket at the counter. Nothing in the numbered cupboard appears in the sale inventory. |

When the player asks to buy the blue coat, Len takes two short breaths through his nose. “If I'd put a price on it you'd have paid me, wouldn't you?” He puts it back folded, leaving the sale rail in view. On a second ask he says simply, “Still fifty-eight's.”

### A man collects his coat

On the way back from the Sixth Jack, a small man in a hired coat has the claim chit in his teeth. He needs both hands to open the clasp at the counter. Len's hands stay on his side of the wood until the man offers it to him. The returned worker was injured on an earlier watch. The defender at the bridge has her own crew and her own journey off the floor.

> **Len** — What was in the pocket?  
> **Man** — A coin will stand in the top one edgeways. Put it in flat, sit down and there goes your bloody boat fare. Gav showed me after he took my money.  
> **Len** — What did *you* keep in the bottom one?  
> **Man** — Half a nutmeg. See if there's any smell left.

Len checks. The little brown kernel has polished a patch in the blue. He closes the chit in his till. The man bought the coat off Gav a winter before he disappeared. To show how much bigger Gav was he stands on tiptoe inside it and pulls the cuffs down. For just a moment they hang right.

- **Warm the nutmeg in your palm.** The man asks Len for a grater and shaves a little into both their mugs. Len sits on the parcel bench to drink. On the next approach the man has gone. The two cups are drying mouth down on the account book, leaving brown rings across the closed cover.
- **Lift the counter flap for him.** The man walks out in his own clothes. Len calls after him to take the long way by the cradles, out of the spray. From a cradle you can see the blue cuffs turned up again. Next time the player asks about the coat, Len remembers the man introducing himself at the door. “Barl. Works the return floor. Went out with his collar up. He looked a lord in it.”

If the player walks past the window, Barl collects his property on his own. Under Shared Settlement the pilot brings in an empty carry sling. Ask after the net. She turns the sling over and shows the wet mark from the bottom of the bundle. Someone has taken the net off her hands at the boat. Under Phased Trunk Release, Barl stops at the dry Net Yard on the way home. The old man wraps two crisp fish backbones in the blue coat's handkerchief for him. Neither man knows where Gav went after he sold the coat.
