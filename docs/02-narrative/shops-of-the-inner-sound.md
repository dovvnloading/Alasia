# The Pig Door

On the ridge above Reedward, Agnes Fenn has taught seventeen pigs to go uphill at the sound of a meal scoop rattling in a pail. When the quay gave way she drove her fortune out in ninety seconds. She went back in for her husband, who was still pulling his trousers on.

She buys tide-hit grain. A sack goes on its side above the pig door. Agnes cuts through the salt-stiff sacking and lets the wet crust fall into the wash bucket. She takes the dry heart out with a child's spade. The miller buys it off her by the measure. What he leaves, she sorts for the sty.

## The second high

Six sacks come up from Low Quay on Meg's cart just as the worker returns from Keld. They all have a tide mark across the middle. Agnes pays the carter the price of spoilt barley. Meg inks her thumb for the receipt and counts the money on her seat. She's a thresher in season. The miller sets his stones turning when he sees her come up the hill.

Haul a sack from the foot of the ridge to pay for either service. A **quick-release back strap** lets the worker drop their salvage on the first dodge and fight unencumbered. Recover the bundle on the way out. Or take **one cart journey** for recovered gear between the outer landing and her yard. Meg can take two bundles at once. A paid trip stands.

**Turn a sack onto its other side.** There are two tide marks. At one seam they meet in a V. A whole stack flooded in the shed would carry one level mark. Both ends have been dipped. Pinch open the seam at the point of the V. Dry barley runs over your thumb.

> **Agnes** — Meg, take these back down.  
> **Meg** — Give me back my thumbprint.  
> **Agnes** — I gave you a price to bring them up here covered. Everybody on the road can see.

Ask at the mill who hired the cart. Corren shows you Meg's crew in last season's threshing book. Six weeks with nothing against their names. Their pay is in the farmer's grain, but the landlord had his brand put on every dry sack for back rent. He left the wet grain to whoever would clear it. Meg took six sacks down to the wash. Corren will buy the contents at milling price and pay the crew after dark. Agnes takes a measure a sack for putting her name on the sale. The money in Meg's lap is only the advance.

## While the cart is here

- **Have Corren weigh the dry grain in the yard.** Corren will pay Meg direct if she signs. She gives Agnes her advance back. Six measures more to split. She signs. On the final return she's off the threshing crew and driving Agnes's pigs uphill for day pay. Agnes will sell you another strap, though it'll cost two sacks' work. Meg hands it over. “He kept my name on the slip. I've got the pay book. He's welcome to read them both.”
- **Take the covered load to the mill.** Agnes signs for the lot. Corren pays the rest into six hands after the doors shut. Agnes keeps six measures and gives the player the use of her cart on later visits. On the final return her biggest sow has to turn sideways to get through the door. Meg has brought another load. This time Agnes wants two measures a sack. “I drove past her door this morning,” Meg says. “Got as far as the top before I turned round.”

Walk past and the agreed sale goes through.
