# The people of the Inner Sound

## The player — licensed field-worker

Siv is willing to take a sack of stones out on the plank you repaired. Tessa is willing to put passengers on it. At South Cut a service hand opens a locked cage for you on the strength of that same stamp. She has seen it on the order that let the stroke through. A hurried worker left a space where somebody could write *Whole route*. A careful one will find their own limit still legible under the duty officer's rider. You owe the people at the hearing an account of exactly what you did, including the part you wish you'd done differently.

Speaking up takes you off the Board's paid list. Tessa has people standing on a landing at home and a ferry stuck in Verran because you rang the public bell. You put your name to the message that sends them back uphill. There's a space for the next sailing. Until Tessa has a hull, you leave that space empty on purpose.

## Tessa Rul — Orren Boat League captain

Tessa can tell when a passenger has been talked into trying a ladder because the people behind them are waiting. She puts herself between that passenger and the ladder and says there's a plank coming. Then she has to find one. The League elects her one season at a time.

Siv knows exactly how far she can make the new plank dip before Tessa swears at her. That morning Tessa has the breath to swear and call the next passenger down in the same shout. At South Cut she and the service hand spell one another on the bench trying to get Siv breathing. When both women stop, Noll asks for the coat with his week's wages sewn into it. Later he takes some of that money to the mason at Low Quay. Tessa still has to go up the hill and tell people what name the mason will be cutting.

Tessa wants one bell's warning in Verran so the two hands at her passenger gangway can catch their launch. Ada has had a whole lifetime to think about what happens to passengers in the time it takes to unload a hold. The two women can have this out and still sit together on the way home. Ada gets cold on the water. Tessa knows where the spare coat is.

## Iven Marr — chief surveyor, Office of Soundings

People have gone down on their knees and felt for a mark Iven told them they would find, and found it, within a finger's width. That's why they trusted his charts after the steps started going under. Every figure he published for Verran was accurate. For seven years he left off the column that named the sinking shore.

At the loading door a porter puts her finger on the oldest of the summaries he has signed. She follows the dates down. “Is that your hand all the way?”

“Yes. Even where the ink changes.”

Renn still needs him to calculate how much ground the new sites can bear. One of his former apprentices takes an independent reading beside him. She asks him to move over. He's standing in her light.

## Ada Sen — registrar of displaced Sere claims

At the Market Steps, Ada knows where to put her mouth to the gutter to make her voice come out under somebody's shoes. She and Kit used to answer the fish buyers that way until one of their mothers flushed a pail of dishwater down on them. Then Ada reaches the rail and has to tell you what else she used her voice for there.

People were already walking uphill. Ada called after them that they'd lose their places. She could see the shore half of the tally in its socket and the ferry was due back. They believed her. Later they let her through to the front when the messenger skiff came for the passenger list she had in her apron. The last time she saw them from the boat, some were still holding the rail.

In Verran she carried bowls in the relief kitchen. At the end of her shift there was bread to take home to her mother. It takes a freight clerk turning up an old receipt for Ada to learn where the rye for that bread came from. She can be glad her mother ate. She can ask Eran, in the hearing of everyone whose children ate there, who went back to tell the people at the rail.

Kit's mother rented the room behind Ada's family oven. When Kit comes to put in her own claim, Ada catches herself spelling her old friend's name aloud. Kit holds out her hand for the pen. Kit opens the inquiry herself. Her own name as a girl appears on the waiting sheet at Sere. A year later Verran paid her first wages under the other surname. She asks Eran to read both aloud. When the clerk moves to cross out *missing*, she holds her hand out for his pen. She knows how to enter this one herself.

## Eran Velt — chair of the Board of Sills

“Keep her level till the hold is empty.” Eran could give that order to six men on any working quay in the Sound and each would know what to do. When you find it clipped to the opening clearance, you can still see the dry socks Tessa unwrapped from Siv's wrist. He was in Verran when he signed.

At seventy-seven he can put a shoulder under a sack. When he breaks the seal on the bonded store in Verran he stays for the carrying. People take the food because they need it, including people with claims against him. He remembers the children eating bread after Sere's ferry brought in the rye. He's been counting those children for forty-one years. He has never been able to tell Ada the name of a single person he sent back to the rail.

Once the scored collar comes out of the Sixth Jack, his chance to bargain with Orren's ground goes with it. He has heard how little packing is left. He can see Edda on the bridge. He thinks he knows how long her mates need to bring her across. He trusts that reckoning enough to ring before she's clear. Maret at the far bell gets to decide whether the old line takes a full draw. At the inquiry he has to sit still while Kit gives him two names to read out.

## Bela Quine — Verran granary steward

Children watch the turn of Bela's wrist as she levels a scoop. If she fills it to a lower mark they'll still be holding out their bowls when her hand comes away.

Bela has spent years talking port hands into taking extra shifts. She's the one who tells the mate to double the ferry's hitch and bring the powered hoist to a stop. The first person to ask when wages will start again is someone she called in herself. Later that same shift she takes the measure from the winter store Eran opened. She'll account for every sack he brings her. He still goes away with his new clearance unsigned.

## Renn Hadr — Selwick repair forewoman

Renn told Eran the reserve had stopped coming clean off the load. Next morning the younger hands watched her to see whether it was safe to go back on the floor. She went. They followed.

Edda is on the service bridge. Maret tends the answering bell across from her. Maret's brother works Verran's grain berth and has asked her to speak for his job. When Eran rings the haul and her bell stays quiet, Renn lets the silence stand. Under the old orders Renn might have called across the floor to see whether Maret had heard. She knows the range of that bell. Once Edda signals clear, the two crews can answer the handover Renn is there to make.
