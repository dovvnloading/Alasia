# The people of the Inner Sound

## The player — licensed field-worker

By the time the trouble starts, somebody has walked across a plank on the strength of your word. You were good for it. You tested the landing under weight, and the woman who took an elbow at the foot of the board is sitting in the boat when the stone slips. She remembers that much about you. Other people have seen your stamp on an order to sink their homes.

What you owe them depends on what you did. If you hurried to the passenger, you left a blank above your stamp and someone wrote in it. If you finished both slips, a duty officer signed a rider over the limit you wrote. You can tell the difference in front of the people whose names are on neither piece of paper. Doing so costs you paid Board work. It also costs Tessa a sailing she counted on. You still have to stand beside her when she gives the missed-sailing notice to the caller for home.

Noll's rig keeps lifting his right arm. His knuckles drag a streak of blood up the grille. “It's coming back,” he says, and this time you see the rig move first. When you get the load off him, you find Siv's hand still keeping his face above water. She tested the ferry plank beside you this morning. You bring her up, too late. Some of that money goes to the mason who cuts Siv's name at Low Quay. Noll pays him himself. Later, with a stranger balanced on a failing bridge, you are the one near enough to lay hold of the working bell. You have to act before you know whose orders the people across the gap will take.

## Tessa Rul — Orren Boat League captain

Tessa can tell which passenger has been persuaded to try the ladder because everyone behind them is waiting. She puts a hand on the ladder herself and says there's a boat coming round with a plank. Then she has to find one. The League elected her for a season. A promise she makes on a wet morning can outlast her term.

When Tessa first sees the valve taking Orren down she goes for a sledge. In the time it takes her to get a swing at it, the pier shifts. People are trying to step over daylight. She lays the sledge across the gap. By that evening she is asking somebody to keep the machinery running for her.

The north ferry is the boat she wants back from Verran. She knows who stood at the head of its boarding line when the departure bell rang, and who escorted them all home. Ada has forty-one years of reasons to insist on publishing the record. Tessa would still have taken another bell. They can sit side by side on the way home. Ada gets cold on the water. Tessa knows where the spare coat is.

## Iven Marr — chief surveyor, Office of Soundings

People have gone down on their knees and felt for a mark Iven told them they would find, and found it, within a finger's width. That's why they trusted his charts after the steps started going under. For seven years he signed figures that accurately described the rise and fall at the receiving shore. The missing column named the shore that paid.

He could let the inquiry begin with the newest signature, still damp from the worker's stamp. Instead he takes his own signed summaries out, oldest first. A porter at the loading door asks if that's his hand all the way down. “Yes. Even where the ink changes.” He stays there until people have finished asking. Afterward Renn's crew checks his work in front of him. On the first reading one of his former apprentices asks him to move over. He's standing in her light.

## Ada Sen — registrar of displaced Sere claims

At the Market Steps, Ada knows where to put her mouth against the gutter to make her voice come out under somebody's shoes. She and Kit used to answer fish buyers that way until one of their mothers flushed a pail of dishwater down on them. For a few minutes on the exposed steps she can be company for someone who never knew Sere. Then she reaches the rail.

“Stay here. The third boat comes to this rail.” She said it herself. She was a girl keeping the shore tally when a messenger skiff came for the passenger list. Ada took the list aboard. She last saw the people she'd told to wait from the stern of that skiff. Some may have got away by another landing. A name on her sheet only tells her who was waiting when she left.

For years she's had to stand up in rooms where people say a bad winter took Sere. When Eran finally admits where he sent the ferry, she has one more question for him. Who went down to the water and told the people he'd left there? She knows what she told them. She wants to know if anybody got there in time to tell them something else.

Kit's mother rented behind the Sen oven. Ada's family deed covers the whole building. When Kit comes to put in her claim, Ada discovers she has caught herself spelling her old friend's name aloud. Kit holds out her hand for the pen.

## Eran Velt — chair of the Board of Sills

Eran was in his thirties when a ship full of food needed a berth that would hold still. He can still tell you what that cargo weighed. He had initialed a list of families waiting for their ferry on Sere. He sent it for grain and ordered the second stroke. No messenger went to the Market Steps. Other people had voted for the first stroke. He took the second on himself.

At seventy-seven he can still put a shoulder under a sack. When the hoist stops at Verran he opens the bonded winter store under his own name and carries grain out to the queue. Ada eats from it. The sight of people taking it steadies him. He's spent forty-one years dining with people saved by the first cargo. He has rarely had to eat with anyone who waited at that rail.

He carries the two rye bookings to Selwick. Under sharing he asks for a landing beyond the twelfth high if a ship comes late. Under release he wants time for the first lighter to unload. By the time he reaches the bell he knows what the crews agreed to. He rings for the old Orren collar to be hauled back into its seat. That would give him time to bargain. There's a defender on the service bridge in plain sight. He reckons her mates can get her clear. When the slack comes tight and the bridge tilts, he has to watch the worker hold the bell still and help her across. The far watch withholds the answer that would send the full draw to Orren. He lives long enough to hear the families' questions himself.

## Bela Quine — Verran granary steward

Bela has an old habit of pushing the nose of the scoop down into a measure, then turning her wrist to leave the grain level. Generations of children have watched that hand. She can give a neighbor the whole of a smaller ration and still see the child waiting for her to finish.

She is the person whose word will set the hoist moving. She reads the berth number underneath the worker's stamp twice. Then she tells the mate to double the ferry's hitch. Half her shift depends on that hook for wages. Before the tide turns she'll be taking food from Eran for people who wanted him off the quay. She can carry her end of the sack and leave his fresh clearance unsigned.

## Renn Hadr — Selwick repair forewoman

When Renn reported that the reserve had stopped coming clean off the load, younger hands were already watching her to see whether to go back on the floor. She went. They followed. Her written warning survives. So does the authority she lent Eran by staying at her post.

Renn can hear the refusal at the far station. Eran's working bell has sounded. The answer that should follow is missing. She knows who's there and how long that woman has worked the watch. She gives her time. When both stations finally answer, Renn makes the setting the shores have authorized. From either end of the floor the crew can see her hand on it.
