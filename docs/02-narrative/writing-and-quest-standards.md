# Narrative craft, quest construction and dialogue standards

**ID:** NAR-003 · **Status:** WORKING / writing guide · **Version:** 0.1

## 1. A quest is a causal change, not an errand

Each quest proposal must have:
- **Human urgency:** who needs something and what happens if we do nothing?
- **Physical location and route:** why is the task here, now, in this weather/tide?
- **Player verb:** fight, protect, repair, read, negotiate, inspect, carry, disable, escort, or choose. Mix verbs inside one sequence.
- **Obstacle that belongs to this world:** pressure failure, blocked ferry, faction obligation, ecological displacement or dangerous evidence retrieval.
- **New state:** confirmed fact, safe passage, altered crew plan, material consumed, changed risk or new relationship.
- **Counterfactual:** if quest removed, what plot link or mechanical lesson disappears?
- **Moral audit:** no false dilemma where one option is clearly monstrous only so the script can congratulate the player.

A one-line quest brief should read “**Because X failed, Y must do Z before W; the player can change Q at cost R**.” A quest that could be transplanted without alteration to a desert necromancer kingdom has failed the seed test.

## 2. Example: main mission treatment — “The repair order”

**Location:** Orren South Cut, continuing A2 → A3. **Emotional pressure:** the evacuation pier cannot remain open if the next surge buckles its footings. **Play expectation:** 15–25 minute authored mission is an early *hypothesis*, subject to graybox timing.

**Spatial beats:** dry entrance safe enough to show the ordered lock → service gallery where a fitting has been installed against its survey mark → first small vibration-triggered enemies → a dangerous cyclic piston readable from sound, shadow and ground seam → mid-fight task to brace a side channel → close inspection of collar stamp → service crew arriving to protect the valve.

**Evidence staging:** a recent inspection date is engraved in a collar that should have been out of service. Official plate names “Anset.” The physical connection runs toward Orren's shelf. The player does not decode an entire conspiracy from a magical flashback.

**Playable decision:** brute force is visibly unsafe; the worker may instead vent short cycles into spare braces, making a temporary safe access route. The mission result never says “you saved the island.” It says **the next pulse has been delayed**. This is urgent and truthful.

**Aftermath:** Tessa is angry that the valve remains intact; Iven orders the gauge plate withheld; the player has an independent rubbable/copyable mark and a reason to travel to Keld.

## 3. Supporting quest briefs that add texture without filler

**“The Forty-Sixth Place” — Orren:** a passenger manifest promises more hull space than the Boat League can actually carry. The player can repair a beached skiff under pressure attack and enable one additional trip, at cost of leaving freight behind. The reward is a changed evacuee scene, a boat crew's trust and a profession-relevant fitting; never currency for selecting who dies. Reinforces capacity and agency.

**“Cold Fittings” — Keld:** workshop crews refuse to surrender casting dies without a replacement repair route. Recover a safe pressure vent from a nearby failed gallery and enable them to release the imprint without crippling the whole quarry. Demonstrates labor tradeoffs rather than portraying workers as greedy obstacles.

**“The Open Roll” — Verran:** Ada needs witnesses from separate settlements to countersign Sere claims before the Board can dismiss them as a single family's story. The player negotiates with an anxious dock steward and secures a protected public hearing. No arbitrary collection of six family heirlooms.

**“Above the Waterline” — Verran:** Bela and Tessa disagree over moving grain versus saving access to ferries. A route-clearing and convoy-defense scene turns the dispute into practical load planning; optional success carries into the ending's visible supply state.

Every optional quest must connect to one of the following: **a main cast change, a production/culture fact, a new combat mechanic, or a persistent visible world state**. No reward-only island fillers.

## 4. Dialogue quality rules

**Speech comes from work.** Tessa describes boats and actual places. Iven differentiates evidence types. Ada names receipts and people. Eran names competing supplies but cannot use numbers to erase responsibility. Avoid generic RPG formalism (“the ancient evil awakens”) or contemporary quips that puncture the tone.

**Subtext over declaration.** A character avoids admitting fear by checking a tool twice. A surveyor's refusal to answer the donor question is more revealing than a speech about guilt.

**Exposition by disputed proof.** No early “as you know, our network...” infodumps. A line earns its place when it changes the player's understanding or a character's action.

**Silence is authored.** Give space for labor noises, a tide bell, damaged stair settling, a rope being moved to an overloaded boat. The dramatic beat can be a count that does not add up.

**Line-edit for human voice:** read each line out loud with the character's immediate objective. If it sounds like a logline or art-book caption, rewrite. Proper nouns must have an intelligible referent on first use.

## 5. Dialogue sketches — **tests, not locked screenplay**

**Low Quay (Tessa and the worker)**

> **Tessa:** Three hulls. Forty-six places if they sit on the sacks. Don't tell the people at the steps we're taking everyone.  
> **Worker:** The north ferry?  
> **Tessa:** At Verran. Carrying their grain. Get this rope clear.

*Why it may work:* physical inventory, class conflict without a slogan, actionable final command. *Risk:* “their grain” can make Tessa sound unfairly anti-Verran; later scenes must prove she knows the interdependence.

**Gauge Steps (Iven and the worker)**

> **Worker:** The sea mark is where you said. The stair is two courses lower.  
> **Iven:** Which mark?  
> **Worker:** Yours. And the old one behind it.  
> **Iven:** Bring me the stone rubbing. Not a copy of a copy.

*Why it may work:* he evades a political answer by insisting on evidence. *Risk:* if Iven already knows all, excessive skepticism makes him cartoonishly obtuse. Actors should play a technician seeking time, not a fool.

**Sere claims (Ada and a clerk)**

> **Ada:** Here. Harbor dues. Paid under Sere, the year they removed it from the chart.  
> **Clerk:** It could be an old account.  
> **Ada:** They raised the rate the following winter.

*Why it may work:* credible documentary contradiction; a human deflection, and a sharp factual answer. *Risk:* the script must explain why records survived and where Ada obtained them.

**Hearing (Eran)**

> **Eran:** We had six days of grain and a harbor that wouldn't hold its own piers. I sent the load south.  
> **Ada:** And then you kept sending it.  
> **Eran:** Yes.

*Why it may work:* he admits a decision without being turned into a speechifying monster. *Risk:* stage the admission only after sufficient documentary proof; silence, timing and reaction matter more than cleverness.

## 6. Scene revision pass

For every core scene:
1. **Logic:** motivation, who knows what, who can physically attend, travel time, documented date and evidence chain.
2. **Subtext:** each speaker wants an immediate, different outcome; remove any line spoken solely to educate the audience.
3. **Voice:** remove interchangeable grand statements; test lines without speaker tags.
4. **Play:** identify what the player can move, inspect, fight, choose, interrupt or witness.
5. **Production:** camera readability, animation scope, VO/subtitle localization and number of unique props.
6. **Ethics:** civilians are not disposable devices; no forced punitive choice masquerading as agency.
7. **Continuity:** rerun [world rules](../01-world/seed-and-laws.md), [history](../01-world/history-and-daily-life.md) and [cast](cast-and-arcs.md).

## 7. Acceptance for scripted content

No scene enters VO or cinematic production without a **beat sheet, location blocking, evidence ownership, character objective map, revised dialogue, optional-state variants, localization notes, and contradiction review**. Leave a line unvoiced rather than pay for mediocre writing.

No quest enters implementation without **completion/failure behavior, save/resume rules, noncombat pacing, accessibility affordances and exact state changes**. A design treatment is not a screenplay; it is a contract for one.
