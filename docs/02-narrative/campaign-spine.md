# Campaign spine — The Third Column

**ID:** NAR-001 · **Status:** WORKING / story treatment v0.1 · **Dependencies:** WRL-001–003 · **Proposed form:** authored three-act campaign, single-player first

## 1. Dramatic proposition

**A rescue becomes an audit; an audit becomes a choice about who bears the next emergency.**

**External plot:** Orren's land begins dropping because the Board of Sills has reopened a concealed donor route. A licensed field-worker discovers a valve whose paperwork identifies an impossible source, traces the records to the lost settlement of Sere, and must prevent a catastrophic renewal of the transfer without triggering sudden failure at Verran's grain harbor.

**Internal dramatic question:** The worker's expertise can keep the network operating, but continuing the work under hidden orders makes them complicit. Can they use the craft to make the cost visible, rather than surrender to either obedient repair or reckless destruction?

**Human antagonist:** Eran Velt has evidence that Verran's harbor prevents wider famine. He is correct about that dependency and guilty of hiding the recipients of the harm, repeating an emergency allocation as permanent policy. We do not reveal a demon or final magical puppeteer to absolve him.

**Campaign thesis:** The difference between triage and exploitation is **public knowledge, consent, duration, compensation and recourse**. The player cannot rescue the dead or restore Sere with a lever. They can interrupt a secret allocation and create the conditions for accountable choices.

## 2. Narrative engine: cause/evidence/cost

Every critical beat includes **(a) physical event**, **(b) new verifiable fact**, **(c) a person whose choice caused or deepened it**, **(d) player action**, and **(e) a concrete cost or changed next state**. The plot advances on the player's acts, not on coincidence.

### Prologue — The stair is moving

**Setting:** Orren, Gauge Steps / Low Quay. A scheduled tide is ordinary; the lower masonry of the gauge stair suddenly shears, leaving yesterday's private waterline above the government mark. Water breaches a ferry approach.

**The worker's entry:** One of three licensed specialist trades is already on the island for a practical repair call. No amnesia, destiny, exile chosen by a god or mysterious artifact. Their craft permits them to go where ordinary rescue crews cannot.

**In ten to fifteen authored minutes, the player must:**
1. Learn a tactile base move while crossing a buckling quay.
2. Fight displaced Bridleshells while protecting a physically usable boat route.
3. Compare two waterlines and hear a character dispute the cause.
4. Choose a *local rescue order* with visible capacity costs, not a permanent moral alignment.
5. Reach a work door stamped with the latest Board maintenance seal.

**Hook:** The stair sank. The sea did not rise. A professional knows the difference.

### Act I — Orren: **The next donor**

**A1 · Hold the quay.** Tessa Rul makes the player clear the Gauge Steps rather than hunt a rumored culprit. **Obstacle:** waves force Bridleshells out of collapsed banks and block embarkation. **Evidence:** private stones show an abnormal ground drop. **Cost:** exhausted crew capacity; some goods are abandoned. **Leads to:** a route into South Cut.

**A2 · Read the cut.** The player enters South Cut to stabilize a repeating pressure pulse. **Obstacle:** failing collars, chalk-colony growth and a rig whose stroke cannot be stopped without timing. **Evidence:** the donor valve has been *opened*, not accidentally ruptured; its inspected collar is recent. **Cost:** a safe operating window is consumed, putting distant embankments under stress. **Leads to:** identifying the fitting's maker.

**A3 · The repair order.** Tessa wants the valve destroyed. Iven Marr issues a temporary hold and demands the gauge plates under official custody. The player can temporarily brace a side channel rather than smash the trunk. **Obstacle:** an armed service crew refuses access without authority. **Evidence:** the order names **Anset**, a site supposedly already spent. **Cost:** Orren is stabilized only briefly and spare braces are consumed. **Leads to:** Keld's casting records and Verran's contradictory ledger.

**Act I irreversible change:** Orren's crisis is public, civilian evacuation has begun, and the player holds physical proof of a manipulated route. The plot cannot return to “a mysterious storm.”

### Act II — Keld, Sere, Verran: **Who was lowered?**

**A4 · The maker's mark (Keld).** A foreman points out a batch mark from Y-41 reused on the current Orren collar. The player retrieves a safe casting impression from an abandoned gallery (not a generic box of quest crystals). **Obstacle:** a fault-triggered industrial accident and a dispute among Stonewrights over releasing the records. **Evidence:** old collars were ordered for a **southern inhabited donor**, not Anset. **Cost:** taking the working impression idles an entire repair line until a substitute is fitted. **Leads to:** Sere's chart coordinate.

**A5 · The absent steps (Sere).** Ada Sen accompanies a salvage trip at exceptional low water. The party has a real tidal access plan; this is not a haunted city that remains dry for dungeon convenience. **Obstacle:** exposed fissures, short access window, a dangerous chalk-colony bloom. **Evidence:** Sere's market steps, survey marks and private tax countersignatures demonstrate *inhabitation at the date of the transfer*. **Cost:** Ada risks her carefully preserved family papers; one companion's claim becomes public and contested. **Leads to:** Verran's hidden Third Column.

**A6 · The two accounts (Verran).** At the Counting Hall, Iven initially releases only observed levels, enough to prove sea/land divergence but not authorization. Ada brings the receipts; the casting die and Sere stones establish the hidden donor. The player forces comparison before citizens who depend on the harbor. **Obstacle:** records are restricted; Eran Velt offers a narrowly tailored emergency hearing designed to delay disclosure. **Evidence:** the signed **Winter Allocation** and the later **Orren diversion**. **Cost:** the hearing disrupts shipping; a dock workforce faces lost wages and rationing. **Leads to:** the fact that **both immediate shutoff and continued concealment endanger people**.

**A7 · An honest alternative.** Iven models a slower, supervised reallocation; the Selwick crew explains that a rapid cut will release accumulated strain into the seawall and lower docks. Keld can supply replacement collars, Orren boats can evacuate lower wards, and Verran can move grain above the historic line—**if each community participates openly**. **Obstacle:** trust is shattered and time is short. **Evidence:** there are two physically feasible emergency plans with different burdens. **Cost:** neither saves every house nor returns Sere. **Leads to:** relief preparation in Act III.

**Act II irreversible change:** the suppressed record is made public to a meaningful set of witnesses. Eran's claim is now “I chose an unacceptable allocation to prevent a disaster,” not “Sere never existed.” The player has a legitimate target and a credible plan, not merely a villain to kill.

### Act III — Selwick and Verran: **The next line**

**A8 · The relief chain.** Mainline tasks enlist actual crews: Orren ferries mark embarkation loads; Keld replaces collars; Verran moves grain to upper stores; Selwick pilots protect the Breach Walk. These are playable short objectives with visible downstream state, not a checklist of four arbitrary favor tokens. Optional rescue work modifies **how well** the plan succeeds, not whether the main story remains finishable.

**A9 · Outer Sill.** The Board sends a protective crew to hold the apparatus under the existing allocation. Its members believe an unscheduled cut can kill people. During the player's negotiated entry, an overloaded routing sequence begins; hostile fauna, kinetic machinery and defenders make the station a rapid multi-stage ARPG fight. **Final combat encounter:** the **Sixth Jack**, an unstable industrial transfer assembly whose strokes alter the arena. Not a resurrected sea god. The player survives by applying the three foundational verbs—read, bear or reroute—to shared environmental mechanics. All classes can complete it.

**A10 · The public setting.** With original registers disclosed and emergency leaders assembled, the player helps execute one of two **feasible, openly recorded** choices. It is a decision about **distribution of physical harm**, not how much the protagonist loves freedom.

- **Plan A: Shared Settlement.** Retune the main trunk to distribute modest settlement to designated, evacuated low margins across several standing islands instead of secretly sacrificing Orren. The harbor continues functioning in limited form; several neighborhoods and workshops must be abandoned. **Cost:** Verran and Selwick residents now bear part of what Orren once carried alone; disputes over compensation begin. **Strength:** regional food movement stays possible.
- **Plan B: Phased Trunk Release.** Gradually discharge Verran's protected lower harbor and end the permanent Orren-donor route while keeping the outer stormwall's separate protective work alive. The lower docks are lost; Orren suffers less additional settlement. **Cost:** shipping and grain storage contract severely; evacuation and rationing remain urgent. **Strength:** the hidden transfer model is ended more decisively.

**Both plans require** safe venting at Selwick and prior preparation; neither is an instant shutdown. Both leave the Board's current legitimacy broken, Sere un-restored, and the displaced still needing legal standing. Consequences differ in post-campaign population distribution, harbor access, travel restrictions, architecture and future political disputes.

### Epilogue — Names on the soundings

The player sees the new chart: observed height, scheduled work **and donor settlement**, names intact. Tessa counts passengers, Iven testifies, Ada files claims, and Eran must answer publicly for what he did. The camera returns to an ordinary working shore post altered by the decision. The last beat is **new civic procedure**, not a triumphant crown or a hint of a larger hidden demon.

## 3. Character agency against plot convenience

- **Tessa** can argue against a plan without withholding necessary ferries out of spite. Her practical disagreement must remain intelligible.
- **Iven** does not become good because he gives the hero a ledger. He must publish his own responsibility and lose control over it.
- **Ada** is an active legal strategist, not a ghostly lore dispenser. She organizes testimony, cross-checks receipts and determines who can represent Sere.
- **Eran** cannot be killed to close the moral question. He is confronted with the difference between a one-time emergency and forty-one years of concealment.
- **The player** does not single-handedly command an entire archipelago. They create proof, win operating access, coordinate crews and enact an accountable emergency decision.

## 4. Where the story needs its deepest writing

The Winter Allocation must not be an elaborate device for making the audience select an obvious moral answer. Eran's emergency reason is real, but the earlier alternatives and forty-one years of continued concealment are real too. The last two plans must each injure a place the player has come to know. Sere's displaced people must be alive, organized and capable of deciding what justice looks like, rather than a memory placed there to motivate the hero.

The campaign should move between violent infrastructure failures, the material culture of working islanders, the tension of testimony, and the quieter cost of evacuation. A manuscript of clever administrative twists is not enough. The player has to make space safe, hold difficult ground and see how their decisions alter the next day. That is how lore becomes drama.

## 5. Tracking flags (illustrative, not code yet)

**Core monotonic flags:** gauge_divergence_seen → donor_valve_confirmed → keld_collar_record → sere_habitation_proved → winter_allocation_published → relief_chain_ready → sixth_jack_stabilized → plan_a_or_b. Each has a clear in-world artifact, scene and downstream change.

**Local state:** boats_readied, grain_moved, crew_confidence, claims_reconciled, relief_losses. Optional actions adjust epilogue detail or encounter support within bounded ranges. No hidden random morality stat and no mandatory grinding to unlock a humane choice.

**The next writing work:** turn the treatment into continuous scenes, dialogue, physical blocking and character interaction. The geography and history can evolve with those scenes as long as their consequences remain legible. This is a treatment, not the finished screenplay.
