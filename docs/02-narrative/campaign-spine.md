# Campaign spine — The Third Column

NAR-001 · Working story treatment v0.2 · Authored three-act campaign for single player

A field-worker signs a ferry landing fit for passengers. By the end of their shift their mark is on an order to lower the island beneath it. When they put both pieces of paper in public hands, the crew responsible for bringing Orren's largest ferry home walks off the hoist.

## Prologue — A place on the boat

At Low Quay, a woman waits at the foot of the gangplank. She can manage a level board if someone will take her elbow. Tessa has three hulls and forty-six places. A fourth, the north ferry, is still in Verran earning freight. Let the player work before anything breaks. A Shorer seats the landing brace, a Sounder checks the bearing by ear, a Linewright resets the anchor. A deckhand crosses with a test load. The worker inks the same worn brass stamp onto the landing return and the dispatch stub in turn. The return names the landing; the stub has its own box for the scope of the inspection, still empty. The runner is on the dry stair, the boarding bell is going and the woman still has her elbow out. The worker keeps the signed landing return and hands over the stub with that box empty. They have done it on plenty of small jobs. Play the loading through one whole bell while the runner takes the short stair to the service desk above South Cut.

Across the Sound a grain lighter has arrived late at Verran's sagging berth. Keeping it level long enough to unload takes one extra loaded stroke. The sixth jack at Selwick is a reserve already doing regular duty. Renn has warned that it no longer comes cleanly off the load. Eran has ordered the extra stroke anyway. At South Cut, the duty office receives the stamped stub, fills its blank scope with “Whole route”, attaches it to the standing Board order and admits the draw. On the first play through Orren, stay with the worker. The player hears the extra stroke underfoot. The signed order and the person who asked for it will come into view at Verran. The north ferry lies in the next berth under a separate freight contract. Tessa has it booked back at Orren on the third high.

The first sling of grain comes off at Verran. On Orren the foot of the stair drops. Yesterday a mason re-scored an old survey cut across the stair cheek and the adjoining bedrock post. Now a finger laid in the groove on the post meets blank stone on the stair. Tessa's passenger still has one hand out as though the plank were there.

## Act I — Orren

### A1 · Hold the quay

Water spills through the burrows under the ferry approach. The player draws the charging Bridleshells off the boarding lane and chooses which passage to clear first. Take the shallow ramp and the passengers who need a level boarding can leave together. Clear the Reedward approach first and a boat can bring up dry bedding before its store floods. Tessa sends a later boat for the waiting group. On the return through Orren, the player sees the price of the order they chose in a night spent waiting or in sheets hung soaking from the rail.

One couple wants to take the cottage door they have used to make a seat. Get them on the ferry first and the door goes with them, hinges turned in. Send the ferry to the quilts and you can take the light end of that door for them when you come home from Keld. [The long board](../03-gameplay/orren-field-sequence.md) follows the landing through both visits.

Tessa wants the donor valve wrecked. A service hand at South Cut has orders to keep it open, signed by a professional Tessa trusted a few minutes ago.

### A2 · A signature away from home

Inside the cut, the worker crosses a gallery between piston strokes. On the safe shoulder they can feel the rig catch twice where the operating plate marks one return. They secure a side channel against the next pulse. A hand who recognizes the stamp opens the service cage and gives the apparent inspector the route order with its stamped dispatch stub attached. Put the stub beside the worker's damp landing return. Both bear the same nick in the brass stamp's face. The return says “Landing only”; the stub's scope now says “Whole route”. Where the order names exhausted Anset, the loaded collar actually runs into Orren's shelf.

Iven asks for the original route sheet. The service hand keeps a copy and the player keeps their own return. By the time Iven pockets the original, three people could give the comparison in evidence. Tessa takes a sledge from the wall. On the slack between strokes, she can see daylight open under the pier foot where her own passengers are crossing. She sets the sledge across the gap for them to step over. Then she asks the worker to leave the brace in place long enough for the boats she has already promised. It is the first time she has asked anyone to keep that valve working.

### A3 · Six high waters

The side channel can take the regular pulses through five high waters. By the crest of the sixth, Selwick must have taken the Verran trunk onto a safe setting or started the staged release. The hand who installed the braces shows Tessa where each cycle will crush the packing. There are no replacement seats on Orren. At each crossing a crew changes the tide board and the next landing looks different. Time advances with the crossing or main job. Optional work takes place on that watch. Reading, exploring and retrying a fight leave the remaining watches intact.

| Tide after the repair | What the worker can reach | What happens elsewhere |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First high | Keld aboard a maintenance boat | Tessa moves the waiting households uphill. |
| Second high and following exceptional ebb | Return through Orren, then Sere by its old ferry approach | The lower ramp closes to carts. Reedward's bedding is already aboard or still drying. |
| Third high | Verran's lower docks and Counting Hall | The north ferry misses its return sailing. |
| Fourth high | Verran relief work and ward meetings | Keld collars arrive on the regular service run. Selwick crews walk the marked margins with their occupiers. |
| Fifth high | Selwick by service boat, then the Outer Sill | The last households leave any ground offered for use. The duty crew brings the reserve under hand control. |
| Sixth high | Last safe handover at the sill | Orren's packing is spent. A new allocation needs fresh authority, and the staged release is ready to run. |

## Act II — The boat that never came back

### A4 · The maker's mark, Keld

The collar carries a Y-41 batch mark under the recent inspection. At Keld, fitters are baking their breakfast on the kiln ledge. One turns the collar rubbing upside down and recognizes the little flaw her old die left in every cast. Getting the matching impression out of a faulted gallery takes the player through a live repair line. The foreman stops the line himself. When the worker comes back down the kitchen stair, a setter is standing at the hatch with an empty bowl. He wants supper on credit. Nis is working her shadow bird on the cloth behind him. The bird starts cleaning its stool in exactly the way the setter does before he sits. [Keld on the first high water](../01-world/island-life.md#keld--the-kitchen-on-the-turn).

His order book names Sere on the invoice for the southern collar. A fitter unfolds the freight voucher that paid for its delivery. The passenger space on the back has been struck through. The number on the front comes from the split brass tally used at the landing. Keld's copy concerns a load of collars, but the same freight clerk kept a numbered entry for each emergency sailing.

One of the fitters worked those boats. He sends the worker back to Orren with a message for Ada Sen, who keeps the displaced families' claims. Ada has already spent years asking for the third ferry's passenger record. Until now she had no reason to look for her missing boat in the grain accounts. What her families have never had is the trip number Verran used when it paid for their boat. It will be on the brass still owed to the quay if that sailing never came back. Keld's crew sends a duplicate casting impression with the next service boat. The worker carries the other by way of Orren. On the return landing Tessa agrees to cover Keld's next repair crossing so its freight boat can carry spare collars. It costs her a sailing she could use for Orren.

### A5 · The absent steps, Sere

At the exceptional ebb Ada pilots the last length from memory. A shop threshold shows above the water. She used to stand on the rail there to see what was for sale before anyone ashore could buy it. The baker’s sill and the fishwives’ gutter take them to the same landing. Send the pilot up to the high sill and she can put a line through the staple for Ada. The lip is going under her boat. If it gives, the pilot will have to drop back to the broken rail and the two on shore will have to reach her by the gutter. Put the skiff in the gutter instead and the worker must clear a way for Ada or carry her line round by the oven. The boat can stay close for the return.

Ada remembers Kit, whose mother rented the room behind her family’s oven. They used to crouch beside the gutter and answer fish buyers from inside it. On Sere the customer’s “Skin on?” would come back “Both sides!” from under his own feet. The worker can hear the trick at the bend while the old foundations are exposed. At the berth, Ada takes a rubbing of the survey cut and marks where the brass lies. Together they work the shore half of the ferry tally out of its socket. Each returning crew reunited the two halves there. This one is still waiting.

Ada puts the rubbing inside her oilskin, copies the passage number and puts the brass in the worker's hand. At the turn the old ledge lets go. A skiff put in at the sill drops back as the pilot promised. Its number can tie the promise made at these steps to the freight office's receipt. On the way back she asks for the boat to lie off for another moment. The corner with the pastry shop has gone under again. Only then does she give the pilot the tide.

### A6 · The two accounts, Verran

Take the ferry past the lower docks before entering the hall. A powered hoist is lifting grain within arm's reach of people in the ration queue. An empty sling comes down wet. Bela puts a handful from a split sack under the worker's nose. This is what Eran bought with the extra stroke, and what a missed landing costs.

Eran has granted a hearing limited to the accuracy of the gauges. Iven sets the casting impression on the southern route in his chart. People at the table can see where the equipment went. Ada puts a number on the table that the freight clerk can find in his own book. Follow three marks in order. Eran initialed the outstanding passenger return while families still waited at the Market Steps. His instruction on the freight receipt sent the promised ferry for grain. His second stroke order is timed **after** he acknowledged the return. The first emergency decision was taken with a storm coming in. At the second, Eran knew who still needed the boat and sent it elsewhere. When Ada asks who went to tell them, he cannot name anyone.

The Board can still characterize the copy at South Cut as a local error. The worker lays down the landing return and admits they sent the stub away with its scope blank because a passenger was waiting. Iven sees how easy it would be to make one itinerant fitter answer for seven years of his charts. He opens the donor register beside his signed summaries, the Orren diversion and Eran's latest exception. Renn's reserve warning sits under that order, already acknowledged. At the table the witnesses see who cleared the extra stroke. Iven has the copying sheets laid out for the bell that calls the docks to the reading.

### A7 · The silent hook

Tessa wants one bell to clear her last passenger gangway. Ada remembers what happened the last time a ferry became a reason to wait. The player can sound the public reading immediately or give Tessa the warning bell. Two north-ferry hands can save Bela's exposed sacks or clear Tessa's gangway and catch the launch home. They have time for one job. At the chosen bell Iven passes the complete sheets across the loading door. Each crew takes a set beyond the Board's custody. The clerks relieve Iven of his rank and rule the worker off paid Board calls pending the inquiry. The worker keeps their trade license. From here on, a crew has to invite them onto a job. On her way back to the quay Bela reads the berth number under the worker's stamp. She refuses to certify the powered hoist on the same route papers. The crew pockets its tokens. As the tide falls, the loaded ferry settles too deep to cross the lip of the berth. The hoist could have taken off the last slings in time for the crossing. By hand, they need another tide. From the upper steps the player can see her still tied up as an ordinary skiff rows past.

Tessa sends the missed-sailing notice to Orren in the same skiff. She makes the caller take it from her hand so the waiting families will know who wrote it. She holds the worker responsible for the day they chose to publish. Ada keeps the record on the wall. Eran crosses the queue, breaks the seal on the Board's dry bonded grain and puts the first sack into hand distribution. He charges the shortfall against winter stores under his own name. The first household eats that evening. When he asks her to countersign a fresh clearance for the hoist, Bela gives him back the pen. She keeps the store open.

## Act III — The next line

### A8 · What people will give

Bela has hands to feed and a ferry to lighten. Tessa keeps the small boats on a passenger-only run, with places reserved for Orren at each sailing. Let the worker carry grain past someone on the waiting list. The person who asks when the big boat is coming is the one who held the other end of the player's rope on the first morning.

A preliminary engineer's map marks Verran's Ropewalk for settlement. The owner lives up the ridge. A tenant draws a little square behind the vats where her family sleeps. The occupiers refuse. Strike the entry in front of them and have the fitters pin its branch shut. That refusal strikes the full freight schedule off Iven's drawing. Under either surviving plan, grain has to travel in smaller lots. No amount of earlier rescue work can buy the place back.

Iven lays out two existing relief spurs at **Verran's Washhouse Steps** and **Selwick's Net Yard**. They were built after the Winter Allocation for short proof loads. Keld's new collars can cap the draw for a limited run. Renn has her crew independently check the figures. The worker visits the Washhouse on the fourth watch. People bring their clothes upstairs wet so the empty lower room can hold bedrolls. Across the Sound, Renn walks the proposed strip with the Net Yard's households while their nets are still out. When the worker lands at Selwick on the fifth watch, one of the pilots takes their sleeve and shows how much uninterrupted floor she needs to mend a single torn net.

Sere paid dues as a Verran ferry ward and had no separate seat when the Board was chartered. Ada will release a household roll for the present relief only if tenants and displaced families can enter claims on their own names. The rented room behind the Sere oven is on her family's old deed. Kit writes her own household on the facing page. She has two surnames to account for and a shift to finish. If the worker takes her end of a sack, she can catch a steward who knew her before she left Sere. He witnesses the two names. Otherwise that space stays open, with a strip from a grain sack to mark it. The four signatory islands accept the roll and give its keepers copies. Standing is won here. Payment, title and the disposition of that room remain open.

**Who can sign.** Under the charter, all four signatory islands together can recall a Board order for a named route and entrust its safe handover to the local watch. Their delegates record the recall at their own landings and send the signed sheets on the relief boats. Renn will hold authority for the Verran handover. Adding any new donor takes a separate agreement. Orren's League commits passage and ends the use of its shelf. Keld's fitters certify the collars. Verran's port hands commit a slow closure of their berth and Bela sets out rations for it. Selwick's crew accepts the staged release as their standing safe instruction. This gives the crews a way out even if no one volunteers ground. The port hands can still object to losing their jobs. They win first claim on the paid hand-unloading shifts and a copy of the ration schedule before they sign.

Sharing requires more. The occupiers of each exact footprint, including renters and working tenants, must agree directly. A refusal bars that footprint. The ward's representative cannot sign for it. Those willing to leave demand dry rooms, removal boats, a compensation deposit drawn on Verran's freight dues, and the right to call for a safe wind-down. Offer these terms at the doors. Sleepers get keys and a copy before the sill can use that shore. A pilot at the Net Yard whose son unloads grain at Verran asks what he will eat if the ships stop. Her sister is rolling up their winter nets. She asks whether anyone in that queue has ever come here to mend one. They insist on the right to stop together or separately, even after the first load comes through. All parties know the sea will keep the floor.

The player chooses which undertaking to carry forward. They may organize the two limited agreements and reduced freight run, or commit their crew time to the discharge sequence and protected hand landings. The delegates witness the particular undertaking and Renn checks it against the readings. If either agreement is withheld, or people are still on the proposed ground, the release instruction stands. The Net Yard crew keeps one copy at the local spur. A runner can reach its watch faster than a message could cross the Sound. Each local watch must seat its own spur for a shared stroke. Back at the jack, two small mechanical indicators show the return pressure from the two seated fittings. Either watch can leave its fitting slack. Before every draw Renn must have both returns to engage the common drive. A stale setting clears with the return of the jack. A missed return puts both feeds on hold while the jacks bleed down. No distant crew has to guess what somebody at the door said. Both routes are available through mainline work alone. Publish the actual names, exceptions, costs and final bounds before the first deliberate stroke under either plan.

### A9 · The Sixth Jack, Selwick

Renn has left the reserve tally hanging on the machine. The scored tooth on its return is the source of the double knock the player felt at South Cut. At the fifth high water the outgoing watch unloads the old route into the handover assembly. Eran's extra stroke left the sixth jack half-seated. Each regular cycle since has pressed the worn tooth harder against its guide. During the handover the watch backs off the other jacks and the reserve finally has to travel its full return. The damaged tooth catches. The next normal draw will drive it hard against the frame. The crew knows the moment is coming. They have spent two watches clearing the working floor for it.

The Board's defenders have orders to hold the current setting. Renn reads out the order to wind it down with both ends crewed. A defender who locked the gate now opens the service approach from inside. Another stays at his station until his partner on the far side signals that she has cover. Shield yourself or go round him. Every profession can finish the encounter with the service crew alive.

First, cross the five working jacks by their shadow and stroke and vent the current load. Then the return sticks. One defender and a cart lane are going down on the service bridge. Catch her and guide her over while the bridge folds, or brace the cart lane while her mates haul her up by the hand. She lives either way. In the first case she joins the carrying line over the steps. In the second the carts make it and she has to signal the final stroke with her other hand. The worker can read the interval, bear the impact or lead a line across it. The last collar will only seat when Renn's crew answers from both ends. At the far station the defender gives the agreed signal. Renn can bring the new setting in.

### A10 · The public setting

At the sixth high, the worker reads back the authorized boundary. Renn checks the far signal, then makes the setting with the watch. If an occupier withdraws after draw begins, Renn blanks both donor feeds at the next safe stroke boundary and starts the same wind-down used at expiry. The reserve bleeds its existing load over the following cycles. Neither site takes a fresh draw.

| Undertaking | First losses the player can see | What survives, and on whose terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shared Settlement** | Washhouse Steps takes saltwater across its workroom floor. Net Yard loses its drying floor and boats carry damaged nets elsewhere. The ground loss remains after work ends. | Only those two evacuated margins take the harbor load. Verran keeps a reduced grain run. The mandate lasts **twelve high waters**. The crews start phased release at expiry unless both footprints freely renew after new readings. Any renewal requires capacity still to be there. |
| **Phased Trunk Release** | Crews discharge Verran's lower docks over successive cycles. The powered quay closes, skilled hoist hands queue for fewer shifts and the winter grain measure shrinks. | Orren's donor route closes. Both offered margins keep their ground. Small boats and hand landings serve an upper port with less capacity. Selwick's gate gearing continues to work against the outer masonry wall on its own circuit. |

In both endings Ropewalk wakes up on the ground it refused to give. Keld has a longer wait for grain. Eran is alive to appear at the inquiry.

## Epilogue — Names on the soundings

At Verran, step ashore into the first consequence. If the shores shared the load, take a receipt to the upstairs washroom. Its holder asks the worker to find the date when the crew must come back to her. If the trunk is coming down, take a sack from the small-boat landing to the ration stair. The scoop is the same size. Bela has filed a new line inside it. On the way out of Verran, pass Iven waiting his turn to testify. Ada leaves her book open on the two claims to the rented room.

Go home with Tessa to the first landing. The shore post now carries the observed height, the work scheduled and the ground that paid for it. A clerk tries to hang the chart at the old height. The woman the player helped board puts his hand a course lower, where she can read it from the plank.

Tessa has an inspection in the morning. She writes the name of the landing across both copies before she hands one over.
